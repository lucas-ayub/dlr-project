\chapter{Results}\label{ch:results}

\section{Setting}\label{sec:setting}

The joint reconstruction (\cref{ch:joint}) is compared with the standard reconstruction (\cref{ch:recon}) and the reference, a
monostatic radar at the full PRF seeing the same scene in the same band. Formation: $\Nrx=4$ (channel~0 on the transmitter),
\SI{500}{Hz} per channel (\SI{2000}{Hz} together), $\bxt=[0,300,119.8,100.5]$\,\si{m}; other parameters in \cref{tab:params}.
Each result is one azimuth line of $12288$ samples (\SI{47.2}{km}), five targets unless stated. The channels use exact bistatic
distances, not the joint model, and no noise (except \cref{sec:solvers}). The joint reconstruction gets only the channels, the
geometry and the DEM, which is the true terrain except in \cref{sec:demacc}. Numbers are the highest ambiguity
(\cref{sec:metric}) relative to the reference peak; \cref{sec:dense,sec:close,sec:solvers} also use an image error.

On flat terrain the standard reconstruction already reaches the reference (\SI{-41.1}{dB} vs \SI{-41.4}{dB}); topography
separates the methods. Runs where the joint reconstruction falls below the reference (by at most \SI{1.7}{dB}) are omitted from
the figures of \cref{sec:simterrain,sec:realterrain}: two of eleven simulated terrains (ramp, canyon), one of eight cuts
(Yosemite Valley) and 50 of 200 random lines; none is a failure. \Cref{sec:height,sec:dense,sec:close,sec:solvers} show every run.

\section{Ambiguity and resolution against the height of the mountain}\label{sec:height}

The scene of \cref{fig:resmountain} is repeated for 31 heights, \SI{0}{m} to \SI{3000}{m} every \SI{100}{m} (Gaussian,
$\sigma=\SI{1.5}{km}$, targets on the surface at $x=-8$, $-0.55$, $0.65$, $2.9$ and \SI{4.1}{km}, heights relative to the line
mean; \cref{fig:heightirf,fig:heightamb}).

\paragraph{Metric.} As in \cref{sec:metric}: the largest value within $\pm\SI{20}{ms}$ ($\pm\SI{154}{m}$) of every predicted
position $x_p+mX$, $m=\pm1,\pm2$, $X=\lambda r_0\PRFop/(2v)=\SI{6.23}{km}$, excluding $\pm\SI{154}{m}$ around every true
target. Checks:
(i)~every standard ambiguity peak above \SI{-25}{dB} on the \SI{1000}{m} mountain lies 0 to \SI{2}{m} from a predicted position;
$X$ is the same for all targets (same iso-range), and $|m|\le2$ suffices since $3\PRFop=\SI{1500}{Hz}$ exceeds the echo band
(\SI{1281}{Hz}); (ii)~grid and up-sampled reference peaks differ by at most \SI{0.02}{dB}; (iii)~the guard is needed, otherwise the
metric reads the target's own sidelobes (\SI{-17.7}{dB}); (iv)~the largest level anywhere off the targets (\emph{floor}) of the
joint stays within \SI{1.6}{dB} of the reference floor except at the three heights the metric flags. The values of
\cref{fig:resmountain} are reproduced (\SI{-4.38}{dB}, \SI{-40.76}{dB}, \SI{-41.44}{dB}).

\paragraph{Ambiguities.} The standard reconstruction reaches the reference only on flat terrain (\SI{-41.1}{dB}); with relief it is
between \SI{-12.4}{dB} and \SI{-4.4}{dB} (median \SI{-6.1}{dB}), and its peaks lose \SIrange{2}{28}{dB} to the ambiguities. The
joint is within \SI{3}{dB} of the reference (\SI{-41.4}{dB}) at 22 of 31 heights and within \SI{1}{dB} at 16. Up to \SI{1500}{m}
it is between \SI{-41.7}{dB} and \SI{-40.4}{dB}; from \SI{1600}{m} to \SI{2300}{m} between \SI{-39.3}{dB} and \SI{-37.2}{dB}
(\SIrange{2}{4.3}{dB} above). It is clearly hurt at \SI{2500}{m} (\SI{-33.4}{dB}), \SI{2600}{m} (\SI{-25.8}{dB}) and \SI{2900}{m}
(\SI{-28.5}{dB}), where a target and the place $X$ away are near a blind height (\cref{sec:blind}; mismatch
\SIlist{0.27;0.26;0.36}{rad}). A small mismatch is necessary but not sufficient: \SI{1900}{m} (\SI{0.20}{rad}) and \SI{2800}{m}
(\SI{0.09}{rad}) are unaffected. Higher relief gives more height differences between places and aliases, hence more blind heights.

\paragraph{Resolution.} The joint keeps its peaks at \SI{-0.03}{dB} (worst \SI{-0.46}{dB}, at \SI{2600}{m}) and a \SI{3}{dB} width
of \SI{5.77}{m}, as the reference, at every height and target ($\approx L_a/2=\SI{6}{m}$): the output is band-limited like the
reference, and the regularisation acts on the ambiguities, not the main lobe.

\section{Scenes full of point targets}\label{sec:dense}

$N=1$, 5, 20, 50, 100 and 200 unit targets with random phase are placed at random within $|x|\le\SI{10}{km}$ (so ambiguities at
up to $\pm2X=\pm\SI{12.5}{km}$ stay on the line) on six terrains: the \SI{1000}{m} mountain, random mountains (\SI{1000}{m}), a
fractal alpine relief (\SIrange{0}{2000}{m}), and the SRTM western foothills, eastern escarpment and High Sierra lines; three seeds
each, 108 runs.

Metrics: the \emph{image error} $20\log_{10}\|\bm y-\bm y_{\mathrm{ref}}\|/\|\bm y_{\mathrm{ref}}\|$ of the focused complex
line, valid at any density, and the highest ambiguity as a \emph{gap} to the reference, whose own level rises from \SI{-63}{dB}
(one target) to about \SI{-30}{dB} (200 targets) as neighbouring sidelobes fill the windows.

(left) and joint (right) against the reference; numbers: image errors.}
\label{fig:denseirf}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/analysis/dense_summary.pdf}

\label{fig:densesummary}

\paragraph{Image error.} The standard reconstruction is at about \SI{+0.6}{dB} at every density: an error as large as the image.
The joint has a median of \SI{-29.2}{dB} (\SI{-34.5}{dB} to \SI{-17.5}{dB}), almost independent of $N$: \SI{-31.1}{dB} with one
target, \SI{-28.4}{dB} with 200 (\cref{fig:densesummary}a). This floor is the model accuracy, not the solver: one target on flat
ground gives \SI{-35.3}{dB} for both methods (the error of $K\,H_i$, \cref{ch:joint}), and lowering $\mu$ from $0.03$ to $0.01$
and $0.003\,\sigma_{\max}$ makes the mountain worse (\SIlist{-30.6;-29.4;-21.7}{dB}).

\paragraph{Ambiguities.} The joint's gap has a median of \SI{1.5}{dB}, below \SI{3}{dB} in 77 of 108 runs and above
\SI{10}{dB} in nine; the standard's median is \SI{27.3}{dB}, never below \SI{19.3}{dB}. All nine failures have a target and an
alias within \SI{0.4}{rad} of a blind height (\SIrange{0.02}{0.35}{rad}); the converse does not hold (nine of the 33 runs below
\SI{0.4}{rad} fail). Density matters only by putting targets on steep parts: the eastern escarpment fails from 50 targets on, the
western foothills never (gap at most \SI{1.8}{dB}, image error \SIrange{-34}{-31}{dB}). The risk depends on where targets sit,
not on how many there are.

\section{Close targets next to an abrupt height change}\label{sec:close}

Two unit targets a distance $d$ apart sit on either side of a cliff of height $\Delta h$,
$h(x)=\tfrac{\Delta h}{2}\bigl(1+\tanh(2(x-x_s)/w)\bigr)$ with $w=\SI{10}{m}$, or on either side of a narrow bump of height $\Delta h$
and standard deviation \SI{10}{m}; $x_s$ lies between two samples of the line ($\Delta x=v/\mathrm{PRF}=\SI{3.84}{m}$) and the targets sit
on the surface; $d=6$, 12, 18, 50, 100, 300, 1000, \SI{3000}{m}; $\Delta h=0$, 10, 50, 100, 300, \SI{1000}{m}. Variants: the
\SI{300}{m} cliff with $w=\SI{1}{m}$ and \SI{50}{m}, and three targets at $x_s-d$, $x_s$, $x_s+d$ (middle one on the edge); 124
runs. Also measured: peak levels, the dip between neighbours relative to the weaker peak (resolved below \SI{-3}{dB}) and the local
image error (\texttt{runs/analysis\_v30/close\_targets\_height\_step}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/analysis/close_maps.pdf}
\caption[Close targets next to a cliff or a bump]{Two targets $d$ apart either side of a cliff (top) or bump (bottom) of
height $\Delta h$: standard (left) and joint (right). Reference: \SIrange{-61}{-55}{dB} in every cell.}
\label{fig:closemaps}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Standard reconstruction.} It depends on the height difference, not the distance (\cref{fig:closemaps}):
\SIrange{-37}{-34}{dB} for a \SI{10}{m} cliff, \SIrange{-22}{-19}{dB} for \SI{50}{m}, \SIrange{-16}{-13}{dB} for \SI{100}{m},
\SIrange{-9}{-4}{dB} for \SI{300}{m} and \SI{1000}{m}, from $d=\SI{6}{m}$ to \SI{3}{km}. With the bump it is at the reference from
$d=\SI{100}{m}$ (targets on flat ground) and fails on the flanks.

\paragraph{Joint reconstruction.} With the targets on flat ground beside the cliff ($d\ge\SI{50}{m}$, at least \SI{25}{m} away) it
is at \SIrange{-59}{-43}{dB} for every height up to \SI{1000}{m}, against \SIrange{-61}{-55}{dB} for the reference here (and
\SI{-41}{dB} for the reference with the five mountain targets, \cref{sec:height}): one DEM phase per place handles the cliff. It
degrades for a target on the cliff face or the bump flank. All runs are ordered by the height change $\delta h$ between the two DEM
samples (\SI{3.84}{m} apart) around a target (\cref{fig:closejump}): for $\delta h\le\SI{10}{m}$ it stays below
\SI{-39}{dB}, for $\delta h\le\SI{115}{m}$ below \SI{-33}{dB}, and all eight failures (above \SI{-30}{dB}) have $\delta h\ge\SI{135}{m}$,
a phase of at least \SI{3.6}{rad} on the \SI{300}{m} baseline ($2\pi b_{xt}\,\delta h/(\lambda r_0\tan\theta)=\SI{0.027}{rad/m}\times\delta h$):
the two targets on a \SI{1000}{m} cliff face \SI{10}{m} wide (\SI{-3}{dB}, \cref{fig:closeirf}, right), targets on the flanks of a
\SI{1000}{m} bump (\SIrange{-22}{-8}{dB}) and the middle target on the edge of a \SI{1000}{m} cliff (\SIrange{-5}{-3}{dB}). On the edge of a
\SI{300}{m} cliff the middle target gives \SIrange{-36}{-34}{dB}, on a \SI{100}{m} cliff \SIrange{-47}{-44}{dB}. The one exception to the
ordering is a target \SI{1}{m} from a \SI{300}{m} cliff only \SI{1}{m} wide (\SI{-33}{dB} with $\delta h\approx0$): the cliff lies inside
its main lobe. Blind heights play no role here: the smallest mismatch of all runs is \SI{0.55}{rad}.

\paragraph{Resolution and peaks.} Targets \SI{6}{m} apart are not separated, by the reference either (dip \SI{-0.6}{dB}; main
lobe \SI{5.77}{m}). From \SI{12}{m} the reference dip is \SI{-26}{dB} and the joint's \SIrange{-33}{-24}{dB} in every non-failing
run. In the 116 non-failing runs the peaks are within \SI{0.36}{dB} of the reference (median \SI{0.005}{dB}) and \SI{0.24}{m} of
their position.

The limit is the sampling of the DEM phase, not the cliff: on a face rising about \SI{100}{m} within one sample, no DEM sample
carries the right phase. A finer DEM grid or one height per target would remove it; real DEMs are smoother (SRTM posting about
\SI{90}{m}, \cref{sec:realterrain}).

\section{Other inversion and regularisation methods}\label{sec:solvers}

Alternatives to the current solver (Tikhonov, $\mu=0.03\,\sigma_{\max}$, two passes) from three papers with authors from DLR-HR
were implemented matrix-free (only $\mathbf A\bm v$ and $\mathbf A\Hm\bm w$):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{L-curve with a reliable search region (RelSea)} \cite{GonzalezCaboverde2026relsea}: $\mu$ at the corner of the L-curve
        $(\log\|\mathbf A\bm g_\mu-\bm s\|,\ \log\|\bm g_\mu\|)$ by maximum Menger curvature, golden-section search on the convex
        part; here a 9-point grid over $[10^{-4},1]\,\sigma_{\max}$, each point a full Tikhonov solve (one or two passes).
  \item \textbf{Discrepancy principle} \cite{Hansen1998}: $\|\mathbf A\bm g_\mu-\bm s\|^2$ equal to the expected noise energy
        (only with noise).
  \item \textbf{Bayes minimum risk (BMR)} \cite{MartinDelCampo2020statreg}: the estimator $\mathbf R_s\mathbf A\Hm(\mathbf A\mathbf R_s\mathbf A\Hm+\mathbf R_n)^{-1}$,
        i.e.\ Tikhonov with $\mu^2=N_0/S_0$ for white signal and noise; $S_0$ from the data energy and a stochastic
        $\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf A\Hm\mathbf A)$.
  \item \textbf{MARIA} \cite{MartinDelCampo2018maria} and \textbf{WISE} \cite{MartinDelCampo2020statreg}: iterative re-weightings
        of the power $b_\ell$ of every place (maximum-likelihood inspired and weighted covariance fitting), from the matched filter;
        $\hat{\bm g}=\mathbf W\bm u$, $\mathbf W=\mathrm{diag}(b_\ell)^{1/2}$,
        $\bm u=\arg\min\|\mathbf A\mathbf W\bm u-\bm s\|^2+N_0\|\bm u\|^2$, at most ten re-weightings. Derived for multi-look
        tomography; used here single-look, with $\bm a_\ell\Hm\mathbf R^{-1}\bm a_\ell$ approximated by its matched-filter value.
\end{itemize}
Scenes: the five mountain targets, 40 targets on the mountain line, 20 on the western foothills, and a distributed-like scene
of 1500 scatterers with complex Gaussian amplitudes (scored by image error). White complex Gaussian noise at SNR $=\infty$, 30, 20
and \SI{10}{dB} (mean channel power over noise power), two draws each (\cref{tab:solvers,fig:solvers}).

\paragraph{Without noise} the linear methods are within about \SIrange{1}{2}{dB} of each other, except the two-pass L-curve (up to
\SI{6}{dB} worse), and the current choice is the best Tikhonov variant; MARIA and WISE are up to \SI{2}{dB} better on point
targets, WISE also on the distributed-like scene. The L-curve has no clear corner (\cref{fig:lcurve}): RelSea selects
$\mu\approx10^{-3}\sigma_{\max}$, which under-regularises, at about ten times the cost; RelSea itself notes that corner-less
L-curves give no guidance.

\paragraph{With noise} no parameter-choice rule is consistently better: the L-curve under-regularises; the discrepancy principle
over-regularises ($\mu\approx0.08$ to $0.4\,\sigma_{\max}$; ambiguities remain and targets lose up to \SI{5}{dB}); BMR is within
about \SI{3}{dB} on most scenes and \SIrange{4}{6}{dB} better on 40 targets, but needs the noise power. The noise itself matters
more: at \SI{20}{dB} SNR the current solver loses about \SI{10}{dB} on the mountain (\SI{-40.8}{dB} to \SI{-30.0}{dB}).

\paragraph{MARIA} gains clearly with noise on point targets: \SI{-41.2}{dB} on the mountain at \SI{20}{dB} SNR (reference level),
\SI{-34.5}{dB} with 40 targets, \SI{-34.7}{dB} on the foothills, \SIrange{4}{15}{dB} better than the current solver at two to five
times the cost, by suppressing noise at empty places. Its prior is sparse: on the noise-free distributed-like scene it is worse
(\SI{-25.9}{dB} vs \SI{-29.8}{dB}), and with noise it creates spurious peaks around \SI{-45}{dB}. The current solver is kept; MARIA
is a candidate for noisy point-like scenes.

\section{Limits}\label{sec:limits}

\subsection{Blind heights}\label{sec:blind}

The joint reconstruction separates a place from those $mX$ away (same Doppler bins) through their phases in each receiver: the
filter step $2\pi im/\Nrx$ (\cref{sec:mismatch}) plus the DEM phase difference
$2\pi\bxt_{,i}\Delta h/(\lambda r_0\tan\theta)$. On flat ground the steps differ between receivers and the places are separable.
At a \emph{blind height} the height term cancels those differences, the phase difference is the same in every receiver, the two
columns of $\mathbf A$ are parallel, and the regularisation leaves the ambiguity (\cref{fig:resblind}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering

\subsection{DEM accuracy}\label{sec:demacc}

The DEM phase error is $\approx\num{9.0e-5}\,\bxt\,\delta h$ rad, so the tolerance falls with the baseline. With a smooth random
error on the eastern-escarpment DEM (\cref{fig:resdem}, four draws per level) the joint goes from \SI{-38.5}{dB} (true DEM;
reference \SI{-39.4}{dB}) to a median of \SI{-30.2}{dB} at \SI{2}{m} rms, \SI{-22.3}{dB} at \SI{5}{m}, \SI{-16.5}{dB} at
\SI{10}{m} and \SI{-10.4}{dB} at \SI{20}{m} (standard: \SI{-7.6}{dB}). With a \SI{300}{m} baseline, \SI{-30}{dB} needs about
\SI{2}{m} rms.

\subsection{Baselines that vary during the aperture}\label{sec:timevar}

The joint keeps only $\Delta C_0$ (\cref{sec:dc0}); on straight tracks $\Delta C_1$ and $\Delta C_2$ are four to seven orders
smaller. To check the suggestion that $C_1$ and $C_2$ matter for airborne systems, \cref{fig:coeffsres} was recomputed with exact
distances for receiver 1 with $\bxt(t)=\bxt_{,0}+\varepsilon(t)$ (bumps, step, drift, oscillation), for the spaceborne system and
two airborne systems typical of F-SAR \cite{Reigber2013fsar} (X and L band, $v=\SI{90}{m/s}$, $r_0=\SI{4.2}{km}$,
\SI{45}{\degree} look angle; antenna lengths and the \SI{2}{m} baseline are representative choices). With a moving receiver
$\Delta t$ and $C_1$ both jump, so the quantity to compare is $\Delta(\Delta t-C_1)$. Since the fits become unreliable for large
disturbances, the robust number is the largest path-difference phase that $\Delta C_0$ alone ignores, computed in time.

\paragraph{Model of a moving receiver.} The simulator uses a flat earth with azimuth $x$, ground range $y$ and height $z$. The
transmitter flies at $\bm P_{\mathrm{Tx}}(t)=(vt,\,0,\,H)$ and receiver $i$ at
\begin{equation}
  \bm P_i(t)=\bigl(vt-\bat_{,i},\ \bxt_{,i}(t),\ H\bigr),\qquad \bxt_{,i}(t)=\bxt_{,i,0}+\varepsilon_i(t),
  \label{eq:tvtrack}
\end{equation}
A place of height $h$ on the iso-range is $\bm X=(x,\ \sqrt{r_0^2-(H-h)^2},\ h)$, $\cos\theta_h=(H-h)/r_0$, and channel $i$
records
\begin{equation}
  r_i(t;\bm X)=\bigl|\bm P_{\mathrm{Tx}}(t)-\bm X\bigr|+\bigl|\bm P_i(t)-\bm X\bigr| ,
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon_i(t)$ only in the second term. Only the \SI{300}{m} receiver moves. With $t$ from the closest approach of the
scene centre and $T=\lambda r_0/(L_a v)$ the aperture duration, the shapes are
\begin{align*}
  \text{drift:}\quad & \varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon\,\frac{t}{T/2},\\
  \text{oscillation:}\quad & \varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon\,\sin\frac{4\pi t}{T},\\
  \text{step:}\quad & \varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon\,\tfrac12\bigl(1-\cos\pi u\bigr),\qquad u=\min\Bigl(1,\max\Bigl(0,\ \frac{t-0.2T}{0.02T}+\frac12\Bigr)\Bigr),\\
  \text{bumps:}\quad & \varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon\sum_{c}\tfrac12\Bigl(1+\cos\frac{\pi(t-cT)}{0.04T}\Bigr)\quad\text{for }|t-cT|<0.04T,
\end{align*}
with bumps at $c=-0.30$, $0.05$, $0.35$. The joint assumes $\varepsilon=0$ (phase map from the nominal $\bxt_{,i,0}$,
\cref{sec:dem-phase}). A first-order motion compensation (MoCo) multiplies each sample by $\exp(+\jj 2\pi\,\delta r_i(t)/\lambda)$,
$\delta r_i(t)=|\bm P_i(t)-\bm c(t)|-|\bm P_i^{\,0}(t)-\bm c(t)|$, the range change of the $h=0$ point $\bm c(t)$ at the beam
centre ($\bm P_i^{\,0}$: nominal track); it is exact only at $h=0$.

\paragraph{Where $\varphi_\varepsilon$ comes from.} As in \cref{sec:dc0-proof},
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\,|\bm P_i-\bm X|}{\partial\,\bxt_{,i}}=\frac{\bxt_{,i}-y_{\bm X}}{|\bm P_i-\bm X|}\approx-\sin\theta_h ,
\end{equation}
so the motion changes the range of the place by $-\varepsilon(t)\sin\theta_h$ and of the reference point by
$-\varepsilon(t)\sin\theta_0$; after MoCo the place keeps
\begin{equation}
  \Delta r_\varepsilon(t;h)\approx-\varepsilon(t)\,\bigl(\sin\theta_h-\sin\theta_0\bigr).
\end{equation}
With $\sin\theta_h-\sin\theta_0\approx h/(r_0\tan\theta_0)$ (step~3 of \cref{sec:dc0-proof}) and one-way propagation,
\begin{equation}
  \varphi_\varepsilon(t)=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,\Delta r_\varepsilon(t;h)\approx-\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,\frac{\varepsilon(t)\,h}{r_0\tan\theta_0},
  \qquad \varphi_\varepsilon=\max_t\bigl|\varphi_\varepsilon(t)\bigr|=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,\frac{\max_t|\varepsilon(t)|\;h}{r_0\tan\theta_0}.
  \label{eq:tvphase}
\end{equation}
the DEM phase with $\bxt$ replaced by $\varepsilon(t)$: the model would be exact if $\Delta C_0$ followed $\bxt(t)$. Against
exact distances (\texttt{verify\_phi\_eps.py}) it is within \SI{2}{\percent} (spaceborne, \SI{1000}{m}) and \SI{5}{\percent} for
every shape up to \SI{3000}{m}; for the airborne systems it overestimates by 3 to \SI{10}{\percent} (up to \SI{22}{\percent} for the
drift, maximal at the aperture edges), since $h/r_0$ is no longer small.

\paragraph{Straight tracks.} \Cref{sec:dc0} holds for all three systems: $\Delta C_0$ against everything else is \SI{79.7}{rad}
vs \SI{4.0e-3}{rad} (spaceborne, \SI{3000}{m}), \SI{47.7}{rad} vs \SI{2.1e-2}{rad} (X band, \SI{600}{m}) and \SI{6.4}{rad} vs
\SI{3.9e-2}{rad} (L band, \SI{600}{m}); airborne makes $C_2$ 10 to 100 times more important, still negligible.

\paragraph{Moving receiver.} The ignored phase is \eqref{eq:tvphase}, linear in $\varepsilon$ and $h$ (a \SI{1}{m} drift at
\SI{3000}{m}: \SI{0.27}{rad} predicted, \SI{0.26}{rad} computed). A drift goes almost entirely into the shift; a step splits about
equally between shift, bend and a non-parabolic part; bumps and oscillations are mostly non-parabolic, so a full $C_0,C_1,C_2$
model would not describe them either (\cref{fig:tvspace,fig:tvsummary}). It reaches $\pi/4$ at the band edge for
$\varepsilon\approx\SI{3}{m}$ (spaceborne, \SI{3000}{m}), \SI{3}{cm} (X band, \SI{600}{m}) and \SI{20}{cm} (L band, \SI{600}{m}).
With airborne deviations of decimetres the suggestion holds in practice, but the cause is the time-varying baseline, not the
airborne geometry.

\paragraph{Effect on the joint reconstruction.} The scene of \cref{fig:resmountain} with the \SI{300}{m} receiver drifting,
oscillating or bumped, motion-compensated to $h=0$ and reconstructed with the straight-track model: the compensation is exact on flat
terrain (\SI{-41.0}{dB} with a \SI{2}{m} drift); without it the model fails (\SIrange{-15}{5}{dB}). On the mountain the highest
ambiguity rises with the drift as
\SIlist{-40.8;-40.3;-37.3;-31.5;-25.2;-17.1}{dB} for $\varepsilon=0$, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 and \SI{5}{m} (\cref{fig:tvjoint}), and follows
\begin{equation}
  \text{highest ambiguity}\approx 20\log_{10}\varphi_\varepsilon - \SI{9.5}{dB},
  \label{eq:tvfit}
\end{equation}
with $\varphi_\varepsilon$ the phase on the highest target: an empirical least-squares fit, whose \SI{20}{dB}/decade slope is what
a small phase error gives (uncancelled energy $\propto\varphi_\varepsilon^2$). A residual of $\pi/4$ gives about \SI{-12}{dB}, so
$\pi/4$ is no tolerance for \SI{-40}{dB}, which needs $\varphi_\varepsilon\lesssim\SI{0.02}{rad}$ (\eqref{eq:tvfit}:
\SI{-43.5}{dB}). From \eqref{eq:tvphase},
\begin{equation}
  \max_t|\varepsilon(t)|\lesssim\frac{0.02\,\lambda\,r_0\tan\theta_0}{2\pi\,h},
\end{equation}
about \SI{0.2}{m} for \SI{1000}{m} of relief (spaceborne) and, for \SI{100}{m}, about \SI{4}{mm} (X band) and \SI{3}{cm} (L band).
Airborne use would need topography-dependent motion compensation, a $\Delta C_0$ varying along the aperture, which turns the
pointwise screen into a space-variant filter \cite{Prats2005topo,Prats2007moco}.
