\section{Setting}\label{sec:setting}

Joint, standard reconstruction and reference (monostatic, full PRF, same band) are compared with the formation of
\cref{tab:params} ($\Nrx=4$, \SI{500}{Hz} per channel, \SI{2000}{Hz} together, $\bxt=[0,300,119.8,100.5]$\,\si{m}). Each
result is one azimuth line of $12288$ samples (\SI{47.2}{km}), five targets unless stated. The channels use exact bistatic
distances and no noise (except \cref{sec:solvers}); the joint gets only the channels, the geometry and the DEM (the true
terrain except in \cref{sec:demacc}). Numbers are the highest ambiguity (\cref{sec:metric}) relative to the reference peak;
\cref{sec:dense,sec:close,sec:solvers} also use an image error. On flat terrain the standard reconstruction already reaches
the reference (\SI{-41.1}{dB} vs \SI{-41.4}{dB}). Runs where the joint falls below the reference (by at most \SI{1.7}{dB})
are omitted from the figures of \cref{sec:simterrain,sec:realterrain}: two of eleven simulated terrains (ramp, canyon), one
of eight cuts (Yosemite Valley) and 50 of 200 random lines; none is a failure.

\section{Ambiguity and resolution against the height of the mountain}\label{sec:height}

The scene of \cref{fig:resmountain} is repeated for 31 heights, \SI{0}{m} to \SI{3000}{m} every \SI{100}{m} (Gaussian,
$\sigma=\SI{1.5}{km}$, targets at $x=-8$, $-0.55$, $0.65$, $2.9$ and \SI{4.1}{km}; \cref{fig:heightirf,fig:heightamb}).

\section{Close targets next to an abrupt height change}\label{sec:close}

Two unit targets a distance $d$ apart sit on either side of a cliff of height $\Delta h$,
$h(x)=\tfrac{\Delta h}{2}\bigl(1+\tanh(2(x-x_s)/w)\bigr)$ with $w=\SI{10}{m}$, or on either side of a narrow bump of height $\Delta h$
and standard deviation \SI{10}{m}; $x_s$ lies between two samples of the line ($\Delta x=v/\mathrm{PRF}=\SI{3.84}{m}$) and the targets sit
on the surface; $d=6$, 12, 18, 50, 100, 300, 1000, \SI{3000}{m}; $\Delta h=0$, 10, 50, 100, 300, \SI{1000}{m}; variants with
$w=\SI{1}{m}$ and \SI{50}{m} (\SI{300}{m} cliff) and three targets at $x_s-d$, $x_s$, $x_s+d$; 124 runs
(\texttt{runs/analysis\_v30/close\_targets\_height\_step}). Two targets count as resolved when the dip between them is below
\SI{-3}{dB}.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/analysis/close_maps.pdf}
\caption[Close targets next to a cliff or a bump]{Two targets $d$ apart either side of a cliff (top) or bump (bottom) of
height $\Delta h$: standard (left) and joint (right). Reference: \SIrange{-61}{-55}{dB} in every cell.}
\label{fig:closemaps}

\paragraph{Standard reconstruction.} It depends only on the height difference (\cref{fig:closemaps}): \SIrange{-37}{-34}{dB}
for a \SI{10}{m} cliff, \SIrange{-22}{-19}{dB} for \SI{50}{m}, \SIrange{-16}{-13}{dB} for \SI{100}{m}, \SIrange{-9}{-4}{dB} for
\SI{300}{m} and \SI{1000}{m}, at every $d$ from \SI{6}{m} to \SI{3}{km}; beside the bump it reaches the reference from
$d=\SI{100}{m}$.

\paragraph{Joint reconstruction.} With the targets on flat ground beside the cliff ($d\ge\SI{50}{m}$) it is at
\SIrange{-59}{-43}{dB} for every height up to \SI{1000}{m} (reference \SIrange{-61}{-55}{dB}): one DEM phase per place handles
the cliff. What matters is the height change $\delta h$ between the two DEM samples (\SI{3.84}{m} apart) around a target
(\cref{fig:closejump}): below \SI{-39}{dB} for $\delta h\le\SI{10}{m}$, below \SI{-33}{dB} for $\delta h\le\SI{115}{m}$, and all
eight failures (above \SI{-30}{dB}) have $\delta h\ge\SI{135}{m}$, at least \SI{3.6}{rad} on the \SI{300}{m} baseline
($\SI{0.027}{rad/m}\times\delta h$): targets on the face of a \SI{1000}{m} cliff \SI{10}{m} wide (\SI{-3}{dB},
\cref{fig:closeirf}), on the flanks of a \SI{1000}{m} bump (\SIrange{-22}{-8}{dB}) and on the edge of a \SI{1000}{m} cliff
(\SIrange{-5}{-3}{dB}). On the edge of a \SI{300}{m} cliff the middle target gives \SIrange{-36}{-34}{dB}, on a \SI{100}{m} cliff
\SIrange{-47}{-44}{dB}. The one exception is a target \SI{1}{m} from a \SI{300}{m} cliff \SI{1}{m} wide (\SI{-33}{dB},
$\delta h\approx0$), the cliff inside its main lobe. Blind heights play no role (smallest mismatch \SI{0.55}{rad}).

right phase. A finer DEM grid would remove it; real DEMs are smoother (SRTM posting about \SI{90}{m}).

\section{Other inversion and regularisation methods}\label{sec:solvers}

Alternatives to the current solver (Tikhonov, $\mu=0.03\,\sigma_{\max}$, two passes), implemented matrix-free:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{L-curve with a reliable search region (RelSea)} \cite{GonzalezCaboverde2026relsea}: $\mu$ at the corner of the L-curve
        $(\log\|\mathbf A\bm g_\mu-\bm s\|,\ \log\|\bm g_\mu\|)$ by maximum Menger curvature; here a 9-point grid over
        $[10^{-4},1]\,\sigma_{\max}$, each a full Tikhonov solve (one or two passes).
  \item \textbf{Discrepancy principle} \cite{Hansen1998}: $\|\mathbf A\bm g_\mu-\bm s\|^2$ equal to the expected noise energy
        (only with noise).
  \item \textbf{Bayes minimum risk (BMR)} \cite{MartinDelCampo2020statreg}: the estimator $\mathbf R_s\mathbf A\Hm(\mathbf A\mathbf R_s\mathbf A\Hm+\mathbf R_n)^{-1}$,
        i.e.\ Tikhonov with $\mu^2=N_0/S_0$ for white signal and noise; $S_0$ from the data energy and a stochastic
        $\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf A\Hm\mathbf A)$.
  \item \textbf{MARIA} \cite{MartinDelCampo2018maria} and \textbf{WISE} \cite{MartinDelCampo2020statreg}: iterative re-weightings
        of the power $b_\ell$ of every place, from the matched filter: $\hat{\bm g}=\mathbf W\bm u$,
        $\mathbf W=\mathrm{diag}(b_\ell)^{1/2}$, $\bm u=\arg\min\|\mathbf A\mathbf W\bm u-\bm s\|^2+N_0\|\bm u\|^2$, at most ten
        re-weightings; derived for multi-look tomography, used here single-look ($\bm a_\ell\Hm\mathbf R^{-1}\bm a_\ell$
        approximated by its matched-filter value).
\end{itemize}
Scenes: the five mountain targets, 40 targets on the mountain line, 20 on the western foothills, and a distributed-like scene
of 1500 scatterers with complex Gaussian amplitudes (scored by image error). White complex Gaussian noise at SNR $=\infty$, 30, 20
and \SI{10}{dB} (mean channel power over noise power), two draws each (\cref{tab:solvers,fig:solvers}).

\paragraph{Without noise} the linear methods are within about \SIrange{1}{2}{dB} of each other except the two-pass L-curve (up
to \SI{6}{dB} worse); the current choice is the best Tikhonov variant; MARIA and WISE are up to \SI{2}{dB} better on point
targets, WISE also on the distributed-like scene. The L-curve has no clear corner (\cref{fig:lcurve}); RelSea selects
$\mu\approx10^{-3}\sigma_{\max}$, which under-regularises, at about ten times the cost.

\paragraph{With noise} no parameter-choice rule is consistently better: the L-curve under-regularises, the discrepancy principle
over-regularises ($\mu\approx0.08$ to $0.4\,\sigma_{\max}$; targets lose up to \SI{5}{dB}), BMR is within about \SI{3}{dB}
(\SIrange{4}{6}{dB} better on 40 targets) but needs the noise power. At \SI{20}{dB} SNR the current solver loses about
\SI{10}{dB} on the mountain (\SI{-40.8}{dB} to \SI{-30.0}{dB}).

\paragraph{MARIA} gains with noise on point targets (\SI{20}{dB} SNR): \SI{-41.2}{dB} on the mountain (reference level),
\SI{-34.5}{dB} with 40 targets, \SI{-34.7}{dB} on the foothills, \SIrange{4}{15}{dB} better than the current solver at two to five
times the cost. Its prior is sparse: on the noise-free distributed-like scene it is worse (\SI{-25.9}{dB} vs \SI{-29.8}{dB}),
and with noise it creates spurious peaks around \SI{-45}{dB}. The current solver is kept; MARIA is a candidate for noisy
point-like scenes.

\section{Limits}\label{sec:limits}

\subsection{Blind heights}\label{sec:blind}

A place and the places $mX$ away (same Doppler bins) are separated by their phase difference in each receiver: the filter step
$2\pi im/\Nrx$ (\cref{sec:mismatch}) plus $2\pi\bxt_{,i}\Delta h/(\lambda r_0\tan\theta)$. At a \emph{blind height} the height
term makes this difference the same in every receiver, the two columns of $\mathbf A$ are parallel and the ambiguity stays
(\cref{fig:resblind}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/pres/res_blind.pdf}
\caption[Blind height]{A target at $x$ and the place $x+X$, $\Delta h$ higher. Top: on flat ground the phase difference in each
receiver follows the filter steps of $\mathbf H$: separable. Bottom: at a blind height it is the same in all four receivers.}
\label{fig:resblind}
\end{figure}

All 13 simulated failures (more than \SI{10}{dB} above the reference, also with other layouts, two channels and other PRFs)
meet this condition (mismatch at most \SI{0.22}{rad}); on real terrain four of the ten failing random lines do
(\SIrange{0.04}{0.17}{rad}), six do not (\SIrange{0.29}{1.01}{rad}), and White Mountains at $2X$ (\SI{0.05}{rad}). Equally
spaced baselines give blind heights on four of eleven terrains and two channels on five (too few independent phases).

\subsection{DEM accuracy}\label{sec:demacc}

The DEM phase error is $\approx\num{9.0e-5}\,\bxt\,\delta h$ rad. With a smooth random
error on the eastern-escarpment DEM (\cref{fig:resdem}, four draws per level) the joint goes from \SI{-38.5}{dB} (true DEM;
reference \SI{-39.4}{dB}) to a median of \SI{-30.2}{dB} at \SI{2}{m} rms, \SI{-22.3}{dB} at \SI{5}{m}, \SI{-16.5}{dB} at
\SI{10}{m} and \SI{-10.4}{dB} at \SI{20}{m} (standard: \SI{-7.6}{dB}). With a \SI{300}{m} baseline, \SI{-30}{dB} needs about
\SI{2}{m} rms.

\subsection{Baselines that vary during the aperture}\label{sec:timevar}

The joint keeps only $\Delta C_0$; on straight tracks $\Delta C_1$ and $\Delta C_2$ are four to seven orders smaller. To check
whether $C_1$ and $C_2$ matter for airborne systems, \cref{fig:coeffsres} was recomputed with exact distances for receiver 1
with a time-varying baseline, for the spaceborne system and two airborne systems typical of F-SAR \cite{Reigber2013fsar} (X and
L band, $v=\SI{90}{m/s}$, $r_0=\SI{4.2}{km}$, \SI{45}{\degree} look angle, \SI{2}{m} baseline). With a moving receiver $\Delta t$
and $C_1$ both jump, so $\Delta(\Delta t-C_1)$ is compared; since the fits become unreliable for large disturbances, the robust
number is the largest phase that $\Delta C_0$ alone ignores, computed in time.

\paragraph{Model of a moving receiver.} With the coordinates of \cref{sec:dc0-proof} (transmitter at
$\bm P_{\mathrm{Tx}}(t)=(vt,\,0,\,H)$), receiver $i$ flies at
\begin{equation}
  \bm P_i(t)=\bigl(vt-\bat_{,i},\ \bxt_{,i}(t),\ H\bigr),\qquad \bxt_{,i}(t)=\bxt_{,i,0}+\varepsilon_i(t),
  \label{eq:tvtrack}
\end{equation}
and, for a place $\bm X$ of height $h$ on the iso-range, channel $i$ records
\begin{equation}
  r_i(t;\bm X)=\bigl|\bm P_{\mathrm{Tx}}(t)-\bm X\bigr|+\bigl|\bm P_i(t)-\bm X\bigr| ,
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon_i(t)$ only in the second term; only the \SI{300}{m} receiver moves. With $t$ from the closest approach of the
scene centre and $T=\lambda r_0/(L_a v)$ the aperture duration, the shapes are
\begin{align*}
  \text{drift:}\quad & \varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon\,\frac{t}{T/2},\\
  \text{oscillation:}\quad & \varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon\,\sin\frac{4\pi t}{T},\\
  \text{step:}\quad & \varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon\,\tfrac12\bigl(1-\cos\pi u\bigr),\qquad u=\min\Bigl(1,\max\Bigl(0,\ \frac{t-0.2T}{0.02T}+\frac12\Bigr)\Bigr),\\
  \text{bumps:}\quad & \varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon\sum_{c}\tfrac12\Bigl(1+\cos\frac{\pi(t-cT)}{0.04T}\Bigr)\quad\text{for }|t-cT|<0.04T,
\end{align*}
with bumps at $c=-0.30$, $0.05$, $0.35$. The joint assumes $\varepsilon=0$. A first-order motion compensation (MoCo)
multiplies each sample by $\exp(+\jj 2\pi\,\delta r_i(t)/\lambda)$, $\delta r_i(t)=|\bm P_i(t)-\bm c(t)|-|\bm P_i^{\,0}(t)-\bm c(t)|$,
the range change of the $h=0$ point $\bm c(t)$ at the beam centre ($\bm P_i^{\,0}$: nominal track); it is exact only at $h=0$.

\paragraph{Where $\varphi_\varepsilon$ comes from.} As in \cref{sec:dc0-proof},
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\,|\bm P_i-\bm X|}{\partial\,\bxt_{,i}}=\frac{\bxt_{,i}-y_{\bm X}}{|\bm P_i-\bm X|}\approx-\sin\theta_h ,
\end{equation}
so the motion changes the range of the place by $-\varepsilon(t)\sin\theta_h$ and of the reference point by
$-\varepsilon(t)\sin\theta_0$; after MoCo the place keeps
\begin{equation}
  \Delta r_\varepsilon(t;h)\approx-\varepsilon(t)\,\bigl(\sin\theta_h-\sin\theta_0\bigr).
\end{equation}
With $\sin\theta_h-\sin\theta_0\approx h/(r_0\tan\theta_0)$ (step~3 of \cref{sec:dc0-proof}) and one-way propagation,
\begin{equation}
  \varphi_\varepsilon(t)=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,\Delta r_\varepsilon(t;h)\approx-\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,\frac{\varepsilon(t)\,h}{r_0\tan\theta_0},
  \qquad \varphi_\varepsilon=\max_t\bigl|\varphi_\varepsilon(t)\bigr|=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,\frac{\max_t|\varepsilon(t)|\;h}{r_0\tan\theta_0}.
  \label{eq:tvphase}
\end{equation}
the DEM phase with $\bxt$ replaced by $\varepsilon(t)$. Against exact distances (\texttt{verify\_phi\_eps.py}) it is within \SI{2}{\percent} (spaceborne, \SI{1000}{m}) and \SI{5}{\percent} for
every shape up to \SI{3000}{m}; for the airborne systems it overestimates by 3 to \SI{10}{\percent} (up to \SI{22}{\percent} for the
drift, maximal at the aperture edges), since $h/r_0$ is no longer small.

\paragraph{Straight tracks.} $\Delta C_0$ against everything else: \SI{79.7}{rad} vs \SI{4.0e-3}{rad} (spaceborne, \SI{3000}{m}),
\SI{47.7}{rad} vs \SI{2.1e-2}{rad} (X band, \SI{600}{m}), \SI{6.4}{rad} vs \SI{3.9e-2}{rad} (L band, \SI{600}{m}); airborne makes
$C_2$ 10 to 100 times more important, still negligible.

\paragraph{Moving receiver.} The ignored phase is \eqref{eq:tvphase} (a \SI{1}{m} drift at \SI{3000}{m}: \SI{0.27}{rad}
predicted, \SI{0.26}{rad} computed). A drift goes into the shift, a step about equally into shift, bend and a non-parabolic part;
bumps and oscillations are mostly non-parabolic, so a full $C_0,C_1,C_2$ model would not describe them
(\cref{fig:tvspace,fig:tvsummary}). It reaches $\pi/4$ at the band edge for
$\varepsilon\approx\SI{3}{m}$ (spaceborne, \SI{3000}{m}), \SI{3}{cm} (X band, \SI{600}{m}) and \SI{20}{cm} (L band, \SI{600}{m}).
With airborne deviations of decimetres the coefficients do matter, but because the baseline varies, not because of the
geometry.

\paragraph{Effect on the joint reconstruction.} The scene of \cref{fig:resmountain} with the \SI{300}{m} receiver moving, MoCo to
$h=0$ and the straight-track model: exact on flat terrain (\SI{-41.0}{dB} with a \SI{2}{m} drift; without MoCo
\SIrange{-15}{5}{dB}). On the mountain the highest ambiguity rises with the drift as
\SIlist{-40.8;-40.3;-37.3;-31.5;-25.2;-17.1}{dB} for $\varepsilon=0$, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 and \SI{5}{m} (\cref{fig:tvjoint}), and follows
\begin{equation}
  \text{highest ambiguity}\approx 20\log_{10}\varphi_\varepsilon - \SI{9.5}{dB},
  \label{eq:tvfit}
\end{equation}
with $\varphi_\varepsilon$ the phase on the highest target (empirical least-squares fit; the \SI{20}{dB}/decade slope is that of
a small phase error). A residual of $\pi/4$ gives about \SI{-12}{dB}; \SI{-40}{dB} needs $\varphi_\varepsilon\lesssim\SI{0.02}{rad}$
(\eqref{eq:tvfit}: \SI{-43.5}{dB}). From \eqref{eq:tvphase},
\begin{equation}
  \max_t|\varepsilon(t)|\lesssim\frac{0.02\,\lambda\,r_0\tan\theta_0}{2\pi\,h},
\end{equation}
about \SI{0.2}{m} for \SI{1000}{m} of relief (spaceborne) and, for \SI{100}{m}, about \SI{4}{mm} (X band) and \SI{3}{cm} (L band).
Airborne use would need topography-dependent motion compensation ($\Delta C_0$ varying along the aperture, a space-variant filter
instead of a pointwise screen) \cite{Prats2005topo,Prats2007moco}.
