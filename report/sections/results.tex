\chapter{Results}\label{ch:results}

\section{Setting}\label{sec:setting}

All the results of this chapter are obtained with the joint reconstruction over the whole line of \cref{ch:joint}, compared
with the standard reconstruction of \cref{ch:recon} and with the reference, a monostatic radar at the full PRF that sees the
same scene in the same band. They use one formation, the default of the simulator: $\Nrx=4$ channels (channel~0 on the
transmitter), $\PRFop=\SI{500}{Hz}$ per channel (\SI{2000}{Hz} together) and cross-track baselines
$\bxt=[0,300,119.8,100.5]$\,\si{m}, a random draw scaled so that the largest is \SI{300}{m}; the other parameters are those of
\cref{tab:params}. Every result is one azimuth line (1-D) of $12288$ samples at the full PRF (\SI{47.2}{km}) with five point
targets unless stated otherwise. The channels are simulated with the exact bistatic distances of every target, not with the
model of the joint reconstruction, and without noise (noise is added only in \cref{sec:solvers}). The joint reconstruction receives only the channels, the system
geometry and the DEM, which is the true terrain except in \cref{sec:demacc}. Unless stated otherwise the numbers are the highest
ambiguity of \cref{sec:metric}, in dB relative to the peak of the reference; \cref{sec:dense,sec:close,sec:solvers} also use an image error.

On flat terrain the standard reconstruction is already at the reference level (\SI{-41.1}{dB} against \SI{-41.4}{dB}); the
topography is what separates the methods. Runs in which the joint reconstruction falls below the reference (by at most
\SI{1.7}{dB}) are not drawn in the figures of \cref{sec:simterrain,sec:realterrain}: two of the eleven simulated terrains (ramp
and canyon), one of eight real-terrain cuts (Yosemite Valley) and 50 of 200 random real-terrain lines. None of them is a failure.
\Cref{sec:height,sec:dense,sec:close,sec:solvers} show every run.

\section{Simulated terrain}\label{sec:simterrain}

\Cref{fig:resmountain} shows the isolated \SI{1000}{m} mountain. The standard reconstruction leaves an ambiguity of
\SI{-4.4}{dB}, almost the level of the targets themselves; the joint reconstruction reaches \SI{-40.8}{dB}, within

reconstruction jumps to about \SI{-9}{dB} already at \SI{100}{m} and stays between \SI{-9}{dB} and \SI{-4}{dB} above, while the
joint reconstruction stays between \SI{-41}{dB} and \SI{-39}{dB}. A denser sweep, every \SI{100}{m}, is analysed in
\cref{sec:height}; it finds three heights where the joint reconstruction is hurt by blind heights.

\section{Ambiguity and resolution against the height of the mountain}\label{sec:height}

The scene of \cref{fig:resmountain} is repeated for 31 heights of the mountain, from \SI{0}{m} to \SI{3000}{m} every \SI{100}{m}
(Gaussian mountain with $\sigma=\SI{1.5}{km}$, five targets on the surface at $x=-8$, $-0.55$, $0.65$, $2.9$ and \SI{4.1}{km},
heights relative to the mean of the line). \Cref{fig:heightirf} shows the focused lines for five of the heights and
\cref{fig:heightamb} the highest ambiguity and the weakest target peak against the height.

\paragraph{Metric.} The highest ambiguity is the one of \cref{sec:metric}: the largest value of the focused line inside
$\pm\SI{20}{ms}$ ($\pm\SI{154}{m}$) of every predicted ambiguity position $x_p+mX$, $m=\pm1,\pm2$, $X=\lambda r_0\PRFop/(2v)=\SI{6.23}{km}$,
excluding $\pm\SI{154}{m}$ around every true target, in dB relative to the peak of the reference. It was checked before use:
(i)~in the standard reconstruction of the \SI{1000}{m} mountain every ambiguity peak above \SI{-25}{dB} lies 0 to \SI{2}{m} from a
predicted position, so the windows are centred; all targets lie on the iso-range of the reference point, so $X$ is the same for all,
and $|m|\le2$ is complete because $3\PRFop=\SI{1500}{Hz}$ exceeds the echo band (\SI{1281}{Hz}); (ii)~the grid peak of the reference
differs from the up-sampled peak by at most \SI{0.02}{dB}; (iii)~the guard around the targets is needed, since without it the
metric would read the sidelobes of the target itself (\SI{-17.7}{dB} just outside the main lobe of the reference); (iv)~to see
artefacts outside the predicted positions, the largest level anywhere off the targets (the \emph{floor}) was also computed: for
the joint reconstruction it stays within \SI{1.6}{dB} of the floor of the reference except at the three heights the metric itself
flags. The recomputed values of \cref{fig:resmountain} are identical (\SI{-4.38}{dB}, \SI{-40.76}{dB} and \SI{-41.44}{dB}).

\paragraph{Ambiguities.} The standard reconstruction is at the reference level only on flat terrain (\SI{-41.1}{dB}); with any relief
it rises to between \SI{-12.4}{dB} and \SI{-4.4}{dB} (median \SI{-6.1}{dB}), and its true peaks lose \SIrange{2}{28}{dB}, the energy that
went into the ambiguities. The joint reconstruction is within \SI{3}{dB} of the reference (\SI{-41.4}{dB}) at 22 of the 31 heights and
within \SI{1}{dB} at 16. Up to \SI{1500}{m} it stays between \SI{-41.7}{dB} and \SI{-40.4}{dB}; between \SI{1600}{m} and
\SI{2300}{m} it is between \SI{-39.3}{dB} and \SI{-37.2}{dB}, that is \SIrange{2}{4.3}{dB} above the reference. It is clearly hurt at three heights, \SI{2500}{m} (\SI{-33.4}{dB}), \SI{2600}{m} (\SI{-25.8}{dB}) and \SI{2900}{m}
(\SI{-28.5}{dB}). At all three, one target and the place $X$ away are close to a blind height (\cref{sec:blind}; mismatch \SIlist{0.27;0.26;0.36}{rad}).
A small mismatch is necessary but not sufficient: at \SI{1900}{m} (\SI{0.20}{rad}) and \SI{2800}{m} (\SI{0.09}{rad}) the result is
not affected. A higher mountain produces more height differences between a place and its aliases, so blind heights become more
frequent as the relief grows.

\paragraph{Resolution.} The joint reconstruction keeps its true peaks at \SI{-0.03}{dB} (at worst \SI{-0.46}{dB}, at \SI{2600}{m}),
and the \SI{3}{dB} width of every main lobe is \SI{5.77}{m}, the same as the reference, at every height and for every target
($\approx L_a/2=\SI{6}{m}$). The joint reconstruction does not widen the impulse response: its output is limited to the band
$\Ba$ like the reference, and the regularisation acts on the ambiguities, not on the main lobe.

\section{Real terrain}\label{sec:realterrain}

The real terrain is the SRTM elevation of the Sierra Nevada (California), where the relief reaches about \SI{4}{km}. Every line is
still a 1-D azimuth line through the elevation model; the map only shows where the lines come from, and the heights are taken
relative to the mean height of each line, which is the height $h=0$ of the processing (\cref{fig:ressrtm}). The elevation is the
SRTM \SI{3}{\arcsecond} DEM (about \SI{90}{m}, WGS\,84) \cite{Farr2007srtm}, file \texttt{raster/srtm90.tif} of
\texttt{github.com/opengeos/data}; a line is a straight \SI{47}{km} profile sampled every \SI{90}{m} and interpolated linearly
between the samples, and the joint reconstruction receives the same profile as its DEM, never the targets.

\section{Scenes full of point targets}\label{sec:dense}

The previous sections use five or twenty targets. To see whether the joint reconstruction degrades when the line is full of
scatterers, $N=1$, 5, 20, 50, 100 and 200 point targets of unit amplitude and random phase are placed at random positions within
$|x|\le\SI{10}{km}$ (so that every ambiguity, at up to $\pm2X=\pm\SI{12.5}{km}$, stays on the line), on the surface of six terrains:
three simulated ones (the isolated \SI{1000}{m} mountain, random mountains with \SI{1000}{m} of relief and a fractal alpine relief of
\SIrange{0}{2000}{m}) and three SRTM lines (the western foothills of \cref{fig:res20}, the eastern escarpment and the High Sierra
cuts of \cref{fig:ressrtm}). Each density is drawn with three seeds, 108 runs in total.

Two metrics are used. The \emph{image error} $20\log_{10}\|\bm y-\bm y_{\mathrm{ref}}\|/\|\bm y_{\mathrm{ref}}\|$ compares the
whole focused complex line with the one of the reference and is valid at any density. The highest ambiguity is reported as the
\emph{gap} to the reference's own value: with many targets the sidelobes of the neighbours fall into the ambiguity windows and the
reference itself rises from \SI{-63}{dB} (one target) to about \SI{-30}{dB} (200 targets), so the absolute level is no longer
meaningful.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/analysis/dense_irf.pdf}
\caption[Scenes with 20 and 200 targets]{Focused lines with 20 and 200 targets on the isolated \SI{1000}{m} mountain and on the
western-foothills line (SRTM): standard reconstruction (left) and joint reconstruction (right), each against the reference. The
numbers are the image errors against the reference.}
\label{fig:denseirf}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[htbp]

target and an alias near a blind height (mismatch below \SI{0.4}{rad}).}
\label{fig:densesummary}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Image error.} The standard reconstruction has an image error of about \SI{+0.6}{dB} at every density: its error is as
large as the image itself, on simulated and on real terrain. The joint reconstruction has a median of \SI{-29.2}{dB} (from
\SI{-34.5}{dB} to \SI{-17.5}{dB}), almost independent of the number of targets: \SI{-31.1}{dB} with one target and \SI{-28.4}{dB} with
200 (\cref{fig:densesummary}a). The floor of about \SI{-30}{dB} is the accuracy of the model, not of the solver: one target on flat
ground gives \SI{-35.3}{dB} for both the standard and the joint reconstruction, the error of $K\,H_i$ against the exact bistatic echo
(\cref{ch:joint}), and lowering the regularisation from $0.03$ to $0.01$ and $0.003\,\sigma_{\max}$ makes the error on the mountain worse
(\SIlist{-30.6;-29.4;-21.7}{dB}), not better.

\paragraph{Ambiguities.} The gap of the joint reconstruction to the reference has a median of \SI{1.5}{dB}; it is below \SI{3}{dB} in
77 of the 108 runs and above \SI{10}{dB} in nine. The standard reconstruction has a median gap of \SI{27.3}{dB} and never less than
\SI{19.3}{dB}. All nine failures have a target and one of its aliases within \SI{0.4}{rad} of a blind height (mismatch
\SIrange{0.02}{0.35}{rad}); as before, the converse does not hold (33 runs are below \SI{0.4}{rad}, nine of them fail). The density
matters only because it puts more targets on steep parts: the eastern escarpment, with the largest height differences over $X$, fails
from 50 targets on, while the gentle western foothills never fail (gap at most \SI{1.8}{dB}, image error \SIrange{-34}{-31}{dB} at every
density). The joint reconstruction therefore does not degrade with the number of targets; its error is set by the model accuracy and
its residual risk by where the targets sit on the relief, not by how many there are.

\section{Close targets next to an abrupt height change}\label{sec:close}

The terrains so far are smooth at the scale of the resolution. To test an abrupt change, two unit point targets are placed a
distance $d$ apart, one on each side of a cliff of height $\Delta h$,
$h(x)=\tfrac{\Delta h}{2}\bigl(1+\tanh(2(x-x_s)/w)\bigr)$ with $w=\SI{10}{m}$, or on either side of a narrow bump of height $\Delta h$
and standard deviation \SI{10}{m}; $x_s$ lies between two samples of the line ($\Delta x=v/\mathrm{PRF}=\SI{3.84}{m}$) and the targets sit
on the surface. The distance runs over $d=6$, 12, 18, 50, 100, 300, 1000 and \SI{3000}{m} and the height over $\Delta h=0$, 10, 50, 100,
300 and \SI{1000}{m}. Two variants complete the sweep: the \SI{300}{m} cliff with $w=\SI{1}{m}$ (sharper than one sample) and
$w=\SI{50}{m}$, and three targets at $x_s-d$, $x_s$ and $x_s+d$, the middle one on the edge of the cliff; 124 runs in total, default
system, exact distances, no noise. Besides the highest ambiguity, the runs give the peak level of every target, the dip between two
neighbouring targets relative to the weaker peak (resolved if it is below \SI{-3}{dB}) and the image error on the stretch around the
targets (\texttt{runs/analysis\_v30/close\_targets\_height\_step}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/analysis/close_maps.pdf}
\caption[Close targets next to a cliff or a bump]{Highest ambiguity of two targets a distance $d$ apart on either side of a cliff
(top) or of a narrow bump (bottom) of height $\Delta h$: standard reconstruction (left) and joint reconstruction (right). The
reference is at \SIrange{-61}{-55}{dB} in every cell.}
\label{fig:closemaps}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Standard reconstruction.} Its highest ambiguity depends on the height difference of the targets, not on their distance:
\SIrange{-37}{-34}{dB} for a \SI{10}{m} cliff, \SIrange{-22}{-19}{dB} for \SI{50}{m}, \SIrange{-16}{-13}{dB} for \SI{100}{m} and
\SIrange{-9}{-4}{dB} for \SI{300}{m} and \SI{1000}{m}, from $d=\SI{6}{m}$ to \SI{3}{km} (\cref{fig:closemaps}). With the bump, both targets
are on flat ground from $d=\SI{100}{m}$ on and the standard reconstruction is at the reference level; it fails when they sit on the
flanks.

\paragraph{Joint reconstruction.} With the targets on flat ground next to the cliff ($d\ge\SI{50}{m}$, at least \SI{25}{m} from it) its
highest ambiguity is \SIrange{-59}{-43}{dB} for every height up to \SI{1000}{m}, against \SIrange{-61}{-55}{dB} for the reference here and the
\SI{-41}{dB} the reference itself reaches with the five targets of the mountain (\cref{sec:height}): a cliff between two neighbouring targets is not a problem in itself,
because the model has one DEM phase per place. It degrades when a target sits on the cliff face or on the flank of the bump. The
quantity that orders all runs is the height change $\delta h$ between the two DEM samples around a target, the joint reconstruction
having one height per sample of \SI{3.84}{m} (\cref{fig:closejump}): for $\delta h\le\SI{10}{m}$ the highest ambiguity stays below
\SI{-39}{dB}, for $\delta h\le\SI{115}{m}$ below \SI{-33}{dB}, and all eight failures (above \SI{-30}{dB}) have $\delta h\ge\SI{135}{m}$,
a phase of at least \SI{3.6}{rad} on the \SI{300}{m} baseline ($2\pi b_{xt}\,\delta h/(\lambda r_0\tan\theta)=\SI{0.027}{rad/m}\times\delta h$):
the two targets on a \SI{1000}{m} cliff face \SI{10}{m} wide (\SI{-3}{dB}, \cref{fig:closeirf}, right), targets on the flanks of a
\SI{1000}{m} bump (\SIrange{-22}{-8}{dB}) and the middle target on the edge of a \SI{1000}{m} cliff (\SIrange{-5}{-3}{dB}). On the edge of a
\SI{300}{m} cliff the middle target gives \SIrange{-36}{-34}{dB}, on a \SI{100}{m} cliff \SIrange{-47}{-44}{dB}. The one exception to the
ordering is a target \SI{1}{m} from a \SI{300}{m} cliff only \SI{1}{m} wide (\SI{-33}{dB} with $\delta h\approx0$): the cliff lies inside
its main lobe. Blind heights play no role here: the smallest mismatch of all runs is \SI{0.55}{rad}.

\paragraph{Resolution and peaks.} Two targets \SI{6}{m} apart are not separated, by the reference either (dip \SI{-0.6}{dB}; main lobe
\SI{5.77}{m}, \cref{sec:height}). From \SI{12}{m} on the reference dip is \SI{-26}{dB} and the joint reconstruction's is
\SIrange{-33}{-24}{dB} in every run that does not fail: it keeps the separation of the reference. In the 116 runs that do not fail its
target peaks are within \SI{0.36}{dB} of the reference (median \SI{0.005}{dB}) and within \SI{0.24}{m} of their position.

The limit is therefore the sampling of the DEM phase, not the cliff: a target on a face whose height changes by about \SI{100}{m}
within one sample gets a channel phase that no single sample of the DEM carries. Oversampling the line (a finer DEM grid) or one DEM
height per target would remove it; real DEMs are smoother than these cliffs (the SRTM elevation used in \cref{sec:realterrain} has a posting of about \SI{90}{m}).

\section{Other inversion and regularisation methods}\label{sec:solvers}

The joint reconstruction solves $\bm s=\mathbf A\bm g$ with Tikhonov regularisation, $\mu=0.03\,\sigma_{\max}$, and two passes
(\cref{ch:joint}). Three papers on regularised inversion with authors from DLR-HR suggested alternatives, which were
implemented matrix-free (only the two functions $\mathbf A\bm v$ and $\mathbf A\Hm\bm w$ of \cref{ch:joint}) and compared with the
current solver:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{L-curve with a reliable search region (RelSea)} \cite{GonzalezCaboverde2026relsea}: $\mu$ at the corner of the L-curve
        $(\log\|\mathbf A\bm g_\mu-\bm s\|,\ \log\|\bm g_\mu\|)$, found by maximum Menger curvature with a golden-section search restricted
        to the convex part of the curve. Here the region is the convex part of a 9-point grid over $[10^{-4},1]\,\sigma_{\max}$, and every
        point of the curve is a full Tikhonov solve (one or two passes).
  \item \textbf{Discrepancy principle} \cite{Hansen1998}: $\mu$ such that $\|\mathbf A\bm g_\mu-\bm s\|^2$ equals the expected noise
        energy; only defined with noise.
  \item \textbf{Bayes minimum risk (BMR)} \cite{MartinDelCampo2020statreg}: the estimator $\mathbf R_s\mathbf A\Hm(\mathbf A\mathbf R_s\mathbf A\Hm+\mathbf R_n)^{-1}$,
        which for white signal and noise ($\mathbf R_s=S_0\mathbf I$, $\mathbf R_n=N_0\mathbf I$) is Tikhonov with $\mu^2=N_0/S_0$; $S_0$
        is estimated from the data energy and a stochastic estimate of $\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf A\Hm\mathbf A)$.
  \item \textbf{MARIA} \cite{MartinDelCampo2018maria} and \textbf{WISE} \cite{MartinDelCampo2020statreg}: maximum-likelihood inspired and
        weighted-covariance-fitting iterative re-weightings of the power $b_\ell$ of every place, started from the matched filter. The
        complex line is $\hat{\bm g}=\mathbf W\bm u$ with $\mathbf W=\mathrm{diag}(b_\ell)^{1/2}$ and
        $\bm u=\arg\min\|\mathbf A\mathbf W\bm u-\bm s\|^2+N_0\|\bm u\|^2$, at most ten re-weightings. These estimators were derived for
        multi-look covariance matrices in tomography; here they are used with a single look, and the diagonal $\bm a_\ell\Hm\mathbf R^{-1}\bm a_\ell$
        is approximated by its matched-filter value.
\end{itemize}
The methods were run on four scenes (the five targets on the \SI{1000}{m} mountain, 40 targets on the mountain line, 20 targets on the
western foothills and a distributed-like scene of 1500 scatterers with complex Gaussian amplitudes), with white complex Gaussian noise
added to the channels at SNR $=\infty$, 30, 20 and \SI{10}{dB} (mean channel power over noise power), two noise draws each.
\Cref{tab:solvers} and \cref{fig:solvers} summarise the results; on the distributed-like scene the image error is used instead of the
ambiguity.

\paragraph{Without noise} (the setting of the rest of this chapter) the linear methods are within about \SIrange{1}{2}{dB} of each other,
except the two-pass L-curve, which is up to \SI{6}{dB} worse, and the current choice is the best of the Tikhonov family; MARIA and
WISE are up to \SI{2}{dB} better on the point-target scenes, and WISE also on the distributed-like scene. The L-curve has no clear corner (\cref{fig:lcurve}); RelSea's search region ends at
its small-$\mu$ end, the selected $\mu\approx10^{-3}\sigma_{\max}$ under-regularises, and the search costs about ten times more. RelSea
itself notes that L-curves without a corner give no guidance.

\paragraph{With noise} no parameter-choice rule is consistently better than $\mu=0.03\,\sigma_{\max}$: the L-curve under-regularises and
amplifies the noise, the discrepancy principle over-regularises ($\mu\approx0.08$ to $0.4\,\sigma_{\max}$; the ambiguities are no
longer cancelled and the targets lose up to \SI{5}{dB}), and BMR is within about \SI{3}{dB} of the current solver on most scenes and \SIrange{4}{6}{dB} better on 40 targets, but needs the
noise power. The noise itself is the larger effect: at \SI{20}{dB} SNR the current solver loses about \SI{10}{dB} on the mountain
(\SI{-40.8}{dB} to \SI{-30.0}{dB}), because the noise that reaches the ambiguity positions is not cancelled.

\paragraph{MARIA} is the only method that gains clearly with noise on scenes of point targets: \SI{-41.2}{dB} on the mountain at
\SI{20}{dB} SNR (the reference level), \SI{-34.5}{dB} with 40 targets and \SI{-34.7}{dB} on the foothills, \SIrange{4}{15}{dB} better than
the current solver, for two to five times its cost. Its re-weighting concentrates the energy on the places that hold scatterers and
suppresses the noise at the empty ones, including the ambiguity positions. Its prior is a sparse scene: on the noise-free
distributed-like scene it is worse than the current solver (\SI{-25.9}{dB} against \SI{-29.8}{dB}), and with noise it creates
isolated spurious peaks around \SI{-45}{dB} far from the targets. The current solver is therefore kept as the method of this
report; a sparsity-promoting statistical regularisation such as MARIA is a candidate for noisy data of point-like scenes.

\section{Limits}\label{sec:limits}

\subsection{Blind heights}\label{sec:blind}

The joint reconstruction separates a place from the places $X$, $2X,\dots$ away, which fold onto the same Doppler bins, through
the phases with which the receivers see them. Between a place and the place $mX$ away, receiver $i$ sees the phase step
$2\pi im/\Nrx$ of the reconstruction filters (\cref{sec:mismatch}) plus the difference of the two DEM phases, which grows with
the height difference $\Delta h$ as $2\pi\bxt_{,i}\Delta h/(\lambda r_0\tan\theta)$. On flat ground the steps differ from
receiver to receiver and the two places are separable. At a \emph{blind height} the height term cancels the differences
between the steps, the phase difference is the same in every receiver, and the two columns of $\mathbf A$ become parallel: the data
no longer contain the information to separate the two places, and the regularisation leaves the ambiguity
(\cref{fig:resblind}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/pres/res_blind.pdf}
\caption[Blind height]{Blind height. A target at place $x$ and the place $x+X$ that folds onto it, $\Delta h$ higher. Top row:
on flat ground the phase difference between the two places, in each receiver, follows the steps of the reconstruction filters
$\mathbf H$, and the places are separable. Bottom row: at a blind height it is the same in all four receivers, and the solve
cannot separate them.}
\label{fig:resblind}
\end{figure}

All 13 failures of the simulations (more than \SI{10}{dB} above the reference, among them other receiver layouts, two channels
and other PRFs) meet this condition, with a phase mismatch between the receivers of at most \SI{0.22}{rad}. On real terrain four of
the ten failing random lines meet it (\SIrange{0.04}{0.17}{rad}) and six do not (\SIrange{0.29}{1.01}{rad}), and the White Mountains
cut meets it for the ambiguity at $2X$ (\SI{0.05}{rad}). Equally spaced baselines produce blind heights on four of eleven terrains
and two channels on five of eleven: such formations do not offer enough independent phases, a property of the formation that
should be considered in its design.

\subsection{DEM accuracy}\label{sec:demacc}

The DEM phase error is $\approx\num{9.0e-5}\,\bxt\,\delta h$ rad, so the tolerance falls with the baseline. \Cref{fig:resdem}
adds a smooth random height error to the DEM of the eastern-escarpment cut (four draws per level). With the true DEM the joint
reconstruction reaches \SI{-38.5}{dB} (reference \SI{-39.4}{dB}); an rms error of \SI{2}{m} raises the median to \SI{-30.2}{dB},
\SI{5}{m} to \SI{-22.3}{dB}, \SI{10}{m} to \SI{-16.5}{dB} and \SI{20}{m} to \SI{-10.4}{dB}, against \SI{-7.6}{dB} for the
standard reconstruction. With a \SI{300}{m} baseline the method therefore needs a DEM of about \SI{2}{m} rms for \SI{-30}{dB}.

\subsection{Baselines that vary during the aperture}\label{sec:timevar}

The joint reconstruction keeps only $\Delta C_0$ of each place (\cref{sec:dc0}): on straight tracks the terms $\Delta C_1$ and
$\Delta C_2$ are four to seven orders of magnitude smaller. It has been suggested that this does not hold for airborne systems, where
$C_1$ and $C_2$ would matter. This was checked by repeating the computation of \cref{fig:coeffsres} with exact distances for receiver 1
whose cross-track baseline changes during the aperture, $\bxt(t)=\bxt_{,0}+\varepsilon(t)$, with four shapes of $\varepsilon(t)$
(three short bumps, a step, a linear drift and two oscillations per aperture), for the spaceborne system of this report and for two
airborne systems with parameters typical of an F-SAR-like platform \cite{Reigber2013fsar} (X and L band, $v=\SI{90}{m/s}$,
$r_0=\SI{4.2}{km}$, \SI{45}{\degree} look angle; the antenna lengths and the \SI{2}{m} baseline are representative choices, not system
specifications). With a moving receiver the closest-approach times of the raised point and of the reference point fall on different
samples, so $\Delta t$ and $C_1$ both jump; the shift of the filter is $(\Delta t-C_1)f$ and the quantity to compare is
$\Delta(\Delta t-C_1)$, not $\Delta C_1$ alone. Since the parabola fits become unreliable for large disturbances, the robust number is
computed in the time domain: the largest phase of the path difference that a model with $\Delta C_0$ alone ignores.

\paragraph{Model of a moving receiver.} The simulator uses a flat earth with azimuth $x$, ground range $y$ and height $z$. The
transmitter flies at $\bm P_{\mathrm{Tx}}(t)=(vt,\,0,\,H)$ and receiver $i$ at
\begin{equation}
  \bm P_i(t)=\bigl(vt-\bat_{,i},\ \bxt_{,i}(t),\ H\bigr),\qquad \bxt_{,i}(t)=\bxt_{,i,0}+\varepsilon_i(t),
  \label{eq:tvtrack}
\end{equation}
so only the cross-track position of the receiver changes. A place of height $h$ on the iso-range of the reference point is
$\bm X=(x,\ \sqrt{r_0^2-(H-h)^2},\ h)$, seen under the look angle $\theta_h$ with $\cos\theta_h=(H-h)/r_0$. Channel $i$ records the
bistatic range
\begin{equation}
  r_i(t;\bm X)=\bigl|\bm P_{\mathrm{Tx}}(t)-\bm X\bigr|+\bigl|\bm P_i(t)-\bm X\bigr| ,
\end{equation}
and $\varepsilon_i(t)$ enters only the second term. Here only the receiver with $\bxt_{,0}=\SI{300}{m}$ moves. With $t$ counted from
the closest approach of the centre of the scene and $T=\lambda r_0/(L_a v)$ the duration of one aperture, the four shapes are
\begin{align*}
  \text{drift:}\quad & \varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon\,\frac{t}{T/2},\\
  \text{oscillation:}\quad & \varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon\,\sin\frac{4\pi t}{T},\\
  \text{step:}\quad & \varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon\,\tfrac12\bigl(1-\cos\pi u\bigr),\qquad u=\min\Bigl(1,\max\Bigl(0,\ \frac{t-0.2T}{0.02T}+\frac12\Bigr)\Bigr),\\
  \text{bumps:}\quad & \varepsilon(t)=\varepsilon\sum_{c}\tfrac12\Bigl(1+\cos\frac{\pi(t-cT)}{0.04T}\Bigr)\quad\text{for }|t-cT|<0.04T,
\end{align*}
the bumps centred at $c=-0.30$, $0.05$ and $0.35$ (zero elsewhere). The joint reconstruction assumes the straight
tracks, $\varepsilon=0$: its phase map uses $\Delta C_{0,i}$ of the nominal baseline $\bxt_{,i,0}$ (\cref{sec:dem-phase}). A first-order
motion compensation (MoCo) to the reference height multiplies every channel sample by $\exp(+\jj 2\pi\,\delta r_i(t)/\lambda)$, where
$\delta r_i(t)=|\bm P_i(t)-\bm c(t)|-|\bm P_i^{\,0}(t)-\bm c(t)|$ is the range change that the motion causes for the point $\bm c(t)$ of
height $h=0$ at the beam centre ($\bm P_i^{\,0}$: nominal track). It removes the motion exactly for the reference height, and only there.

\paragraph{Where $\varphi_\varepsilon$ comes from.} The derivative of the receiver range with respect to its cross-track position is
the cross-track component of the unit line of sight,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\,|\bm P_i-\bm X|}{\partial\,\bxt_{,i}}=\frac{\bxt_{,i}-y_{\bm X}}{|\bm P_i-\bm X|}\approx-\sin\theta_h ,
\end{equation}
so to first order in $\varepsilon/r_0$ the motion changes the range of the place by $-\varepsilon(t)\sin\theta_h$, and that of the reference
point by $-\varepsilon(t)\sin\theta_0$. The MoCo removes the second; what remains on the place is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta r_\varepsilon(t;h)\approx-\varepsilon(t)\,\bigl(\sin\theta_h-\sin\theta_0\bigr).
\end{equation}
On the iso-range, $\cos\theta_h=(H-h)/r_0$ gives $\mathrm d\theta/\mathrm dh=1/(r_0\sin\theta)$ and therefore
$\mathrm d(\sin\theta)/\mathrm dh=\cos\theta/(r_0\sin\theta)=1/(r_0\tan\theta)$; for $h\ll r_0$, $\sin\theta_h-\sin\theta_0\approx h/(r_0\tan\theta_0)$.
Since only the receiver moves, the path changes once (one way), and the phase left on the place is
\begin{equation}
  \varphi_\varepsilon(t)=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,\Delta r_\varepsilon(t;h)\approx-\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,\frac{\varepsilon(t)\,h}{r_0\tan\theta_0},
  \qquad \varphi_\varepsilon=\max_t\bigl|\varphi_\varepsilon(t)\bigr|=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,\frac{\max_t|\varepsilon(t)|\;h}{r_0\tan\theta_0}.
  \label{eq:tvphase}
\end{equation}
It is the DEM phase of \cref{sec:dc0} with the baseline $\bxt$ replaced by its change $\varepsilon(t)$: the model would be exact if
$\Delta C_0$ followed $\bxt(t)$ along the aperture. Against the exact distances (script \texttt{verify\_phi\_eps.py}) the law is within
\SI{2}{\percent} for the spaceborne system at \SI{1000}{m} and within \SI{5}{\percent} for every shape and height up to \SI{3000}{m}; for the
airborne systems it overestimates by 3 to \SI{10}{\percent} (up to \SI{22}{\percent} for the drift, whose largest value falls at the edges of the
aperture, where the line of sight is turned along track), because $h/r_0$ is no longer small.

\paragraph{Straight tracks.} The statement of \cref{sec:dc0} holds for all three systems. At the highest height the $\Delta C_0$
phase against everything else together is \SI{79.7}{rad} against \SI{4.0e-3}{rad} (spaceborne, \SI{3000}{m}), \SI{47.7}{rad} against
\SI{2.1e-2}{rad} (X band, \SI{600}{m}) and \SI{6.4}{rad} against \SI{3.9e-2}{rad} (L band, \SI{600}{m}): the airborne geometry makes
$C_2$ relatively 10 to 100 times more important, but still negligible.

\paragraph{Moving receiver.} The phase that $\Delta C_0$ alone ignores is \eqref{eq:tvphase}: it grows linearly with the change of
the baseline and with the height (a drift of \SI{1}{m} at \SI{3000}{m} gives \SI{0.27}{rad} predicted and
\SI{0.26}{rad} computed). A drift goes almost entirely into the shift $\Delta(\Delta t-C_1)$; a step splits about equally between the shift,
the bend and a part that is not a parabola; bumps and oscillations are mostly not a parabola, so a model with all of $C_0$, $C_1$ and
$C_2$ would not describe them either (\cref{fig:tvspace,fig:tvsummary}). The phase reaches $\pi/4$ at the band edge for
$\varepsilon\approx\SI{3}{m}$ (spaceborne, \SI{3000}{m}), \SI{3}{cm} (X band, \SI{600}{m}) and \SI{20}{cm} (L band, \SI{600}{m}).
Since airborne motion deviations of decimetres are common, the remark is right in practice, but the cause is the baseline that varies in
time, not the airborne geometry itself.

\paragraph{Effect on the joint reconstruction.} The scene of \cref{fig:resmountain} was simulated with the \SI{300}{m} receiver on a
drifting, oscillating or bumped track, the channels were motion-compensated to the reference height ($h=0$, line of sight at the beam
centre) and then reconstructed with the model of straight tracks. The compensation is exact on flat terrain (\SI{-41.0}{dB} with a
\SI{2}{m} drift); without it the model fails (\SIrange{-15}{5}{dB}). On the mountain the highest ambiguity rises with the drift as
\SIlist{-40.8;-40.3;-37.3;-31.5;-25.2;-17.1}{dB} for $\varepsilon=0$, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 and \SI{5}{m} (\cref{fig:tvjoint}), and follows
\begin{equation}
  \text{highest ambiguity}\approx 20\log_{10}\varphi_\varepsilon - \SI{9.5}{dB},
  \label{eq:tvfit}
\end{equation}
with $\varphi_\varepsilon$ the phase left on the highest target. This is an empirical least-squares fit to the runs of \cref{fig:tvjoint}, not
a derived law; its slope of \SI{20}{dB} per decade is what a small phase error gives, since the energy that is not cancelled grows as
$\varphi_\varepsilon^2$. A residual of $\pi/4$ therefore gives about \SI{-12}{dB}: $\pi/4$ is not a
tolerance for an ambiguity suppression of \SI{-40}{dB}, which needs $\varphi_\varepsilon\lesssim\SI{0.02}{rad}$ (\eqref{eq:tvfit} gives \SI{-43.5}{dB} there). Solving \eqref{eq:tvphase} for
$\varepsilon$,
\begin{equation}
  \max_t|\varepsilon(t)|\lesssim\frac{0.02\,\lambda\,r_0\tan\theta_0}{2\pi\,h},
\end{equation}
which allows an uncompensated baseline change of about \SI{0.2}{m} for \SI{1000}{m} of relief in the spaceborne case, and, for
\SI{100}{m} of relief, about \SI{4}{mm} in X band and \SI{3}{cm} in L band. Airborne use of the method would thus need a
topography-dependent motion compensation, i.e.\ a $\Delta C_0$ that changes along the aperture, which turns the pointwise screen of the
model into a space-variant filter, the problem studied for airborne interferometry in \cite{Prats2005topo,Prats2007moco}.
