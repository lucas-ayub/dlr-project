\chapter{Results}\label{ch:results}

All numbers in this chapter are the highest ambiguity of \cref{sec:metric}, in dB relative to the peak of the reference.
The joint reconstruction receives only the system parameters and the DEM. The scenes cover along-track ramps (\SIrange{3}{20}{\degree}),
sines and cosines of several periods, and realistic terrains (hills, mountains, escarpments, plateaus, fractal alpine relief,
glacial valleys, ridges, canyons, volcanoes), each with one, a few and many point targets (up to 49). The baselines are
$\Nrx=2,3,4,6,8$ with $\bxt^{\max}=\SIlist{100;300;500}{m}$ (random, the largest exactly $\bxt^{\max}$), plus five fixed
four-channel sets: two random ones (up to \SI{100}{m} and up to \SI{500}{m}), two equally spaced ones ($[0,10,20,30]$ and
$[0,50,100,150]$\,\si{m}) and one with baselines on both sides of the transmitter ($[0,-50,50,100]$\,\si{m}). Channel~0 is always the transmitter.

\section{Sweep of the joint reconstruction in 1-D}\label{sec:sweep1d}

The complete 1-D sweep runs the reference, the uncorrected reconstruction and
the joint reconstruction over the whole line on \SweepOneScenes{} scenes $\times$ 20 baseline sets = \SweepOneRuns{} runs.
The medians are \SI{\SweepOneMedRef}{dB} (reference), \SI{\SweepOneMedNone}{dB} (no correction) and
\SI{\SweepOneMedJoint}{dB} (joint); \SweepOneWithinTen\,\% of the runs are within \SI{10}{dB} of the reference.
\Cref{fig:nrx1d} splits the result by the number of channels and \cref{fig:scatter} shows every run.

With random cross-track baselines and three or more channels the joint reconstruction reaches the level of the reference
in almost every run: of the \SweepOneUnresolved{} runs that stay above \SI{-30}{dB} and more than \SI{10}{dB} from the
reference, only 5 belong to these formations, while the others have two channels (53) or the equally spaced baselines
$[0,50,100,150]$\,\si{m} (25), which give the channels too little phase diversity to separate the parcels (\cref{sec:mismatch}).
\Cref{fig:jointnrx} shows the role of the number of channels on one scene with 49 targets on random mountains and
$\bxt^{\max}=\SI{300}{m}$: with three, four and eight channels the joint reconstruction is within \SIrange{1}{4}{dB} of the
reference, and with two channels it still lowers the ambiguities to \SI{-22.3}{dB} (reference \SI{-38.5}{dB}).

\section{Sweep of the joint reconstruction in 2-D}\label{sec:sweep2d}

The 2-D sweep uses \SweepTwoScenes{} 3-D scenes (ramps along azimuth, range
and oblique; sines and cosines; realistic and fractal terrains without layover; one target on a flank, five on a diagonal or a
$7\times7$ grid) with the same 20 baseline sets, \SweepTwoRuns{} runs in total. The medians are \SI{\SweepTwoMedRef}{dB}
(reference), \SI{\SweepTwoMedNone}{dB} (no correction) and \SI{\SweepTwoMedJoint}{dB} (joint), all in the common range band.
In every run the joint reconstruction is at least as good as the uncorrected one.

\section{Accuracy of the DEM and noise}\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{DEM accuracy.} The phase error is $\approx\num{9.0e-5}\,\bxt\,\delta h$ rad, so the DEM accuracy needed grows
with the baseline (\cref{fig:demerror}). With an rms height error of \SI{2}{m} the joint reconstruction keeps
\SI{-43.6}{dB} with $\bxt^{\max}=\SI{135}{m}$, and with \SI{5}{m} it reaches \SI{-36}{dB}; for $\bxt^{\max}=\SI{300}{m}$ a
DEM of about \SI{2}{m} keeps it near \SI{-29}{dB}.

\paragraph{Noise.} Relative to the standard reconstruction, the joint reconstruction changes the output noise by
\SIrange{0.3}{4}{dB} with three or more channels and random baselines; the regularisation keeps it bounded in every
configuration.

\paragraph{Formation design.} The channels separate the parcels best when they see them with different phases: three or
more channels and cross-track baselines that are not equally spaced (\cref{sec:mismatch}). This is a simple rule for the
design of a formation that uses the joint reconstruction.
