\chapter{Conclusions and outlook}\label{ch:conclusions}

\section{Conclusions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item With cross-track baselines, a height $h$ adds to channel $i$ the phase
        $\varphi_i\approx 2\pi\bxt_{,i}h/(\lambda r_0\tan\theta)$, which the standard reconstruction cannot absorb: on a
        \SI{1000}{m} mountain its highest ambiguity rises from \SI{-41}{dB} (flat) to \SI{-4.4}{dB}; on real terrain its
        median is \SI{-6.2}{dB} (reference near \SI{-40}{dB}).
  \item A per-channel correction gives one phase to positions $X\approx\SI{6.2}{km}$ apart that share a Doppler bin. The
        joint reconstruction, with the DEM phase inside a matrix-free regularised inversion, reaches \SI{-40.8}{dB} on the
        mountain (reference \SI{-41.4}{dB}) and is within \SI{3}{dB} of the reference on seven of nine simulated terrains,
        six of seven real cuts and 130 of 150 random lines, never worse than the standard reconstruction.
  \item Failures are blind heights (a place and the place $X$ away get the same phase difference in every receiver): all
        simulated failures and four of the ten failing real lines; six are not explained yet.
  \item Over mountain heights up to \SI{3}{km} (every \SI{100}{m}) it is within \SI{3}{dB} at 22 of 31 heights, hurt only at
        three near blind heights, with the reference \SI{3}{dB} width (\SI{5.77}{m}).
  \item With 1 to 200 targets per line on six terrains the image error stays near \SI{-30}{dB} (accuracy of $K\,H_i$);
        the 9 failures of 108 runs involve a target near a blind height.
  \item Two targets on either side of a cliff up to \SI{1000}{m} stay at \SIrange{-59}{-43}{dB}; only a target on the cliff
        face, with more than about \SI{100}{m} of height change between two DEM samples (\SI{3.84}{m}), degrades it.
  \item DEM accuracy: about \SI{2}{m} rms of error gives \SI{-30}{dB} with a \SI{300}{m} baseline.
  \item On straight tracks only $\Delta C_0$ matters, also airborne. A baseline change $\varepsilon(t)$ adds the phase
        $2\pi\varepsilon(t)\,h/(\lambda r_0\tan\theta)$; after motion compensation to $h=0$ the ambiguity follows
        $20\log_{10}\varphi-\SI{9.5}{dB}$, so \SI{-40}{dB} needs residual phases below about \SI{0.02}{rad}.
  \item Without noise no tested parameter-choice rule (L-curve with RelSea, discrepancy, Bayes minimum risk) beats
        $\mu=0.03\,\sigma_{\max}$; at \SI{20}{dB} SNR the current solver loses about \SI{10}{dB} on the mountain, which MARIA
        recovers on point-target scenes at two to five times the cost.
\end{itemize}

\section{Outlook}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Not yet tested: real antenna patterns, orbits, layover, distributed scenes and real data; noise only in
        \cref{sec:solvers}; moving baselines only with first-order MoCo (\cref{sec:timevar}).
  \item Topography-dependent motion compensation ($\Delta C_0$ varying along the aperture), in particular for airborne
        formations; MARIA-type regularisation for noisy data, with safeguards for distributed scenes.
  \item Two dimensions (azimuth and range); DEM correction from the unexplained part of the channels; the blind-height
        condition (\cref{sec:blind}) as a baseline design rule; block-wise or GPU implementation.
\end{itemize}
