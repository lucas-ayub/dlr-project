\chapter{Conclusions and outlook}\label{ch:conclusions}

\section{Conclusions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Cross-track baselines turn topography into a channel-dependent phase that the standard multichannel
        reconstruction cannot absorb, $\varphi_i\approx 2\pi\bxt_{,i}h/(\lambda r_0\tan\theta)$. On a \SI{1000}{m} mountain the
        highest ambiguity of the standard reconstruction rises from \SI{-41}{dB} on flat terrain to \SI{-4.4}{dB}; on real terrain
        its median is \SI{-6.2}{dB}, against a reference near \SI{-40}{dB}.
  \item The right phase can be read from the DEM, but a correction applied to each aliased channel before the reconstruction
        can give only one phase to the parcels of positions $X\approx\SI{6.2}{km}$ apart that share a Doppler bin.
        Placing the DEM phase inside the inversion solves this. The joint reconstruction over the whole line models every
        channel from the focused line and the DEM and inverts it by regularised least squares, without ever forming the matrix
        of the model. Using only the system parameters and the DEM it reaches \SI{-40.8}{dB} on the \SI{1000}{m} mountain
        (reference \SI{-41.4}{dB}), is within \SI{3}{dB} of the reference on seven of nine simulated terrains, on six of seven
        real-terrain cuts and on 130 of 150 random real-terrain lines, and is never worse than the standard reconstruction.
  \item The failures are blind heights: a place and the place $X$ away get the same phase difference in every receiver, and the
        data do not contain the information to separate them. All simulated failures and four of the ten failing real lines meet
        this condition; the other six are not explained yet. The regularisation leaves the ambiguity there instead of amplifying
        noise.
  \item On a mountain swept every \SI{100}{m} up to \SI{3}{km} the joint reconstruction is within \SI{3}{dB} of the reference at 22 of
        31 heights; the three heights where it is clearly hurt are near blind heights. It keeps the target peaks and the \SI{3}{dB} width
        of the reference (\SI{5.77}{m}): it does not cost resolution.
  \item With 1 to 200 targets per line on six terrains its image error is about \SI{-30}{dB} at every density, set by the accuracy of the
        model $K\,H_i$; the failures (9 of 108 runs) all involve a target near a blind height.
  \item An abrupt height change between close targets is not a problem in itself: two targets on either side of a cliff up to
    \SI{1000}{m} high stay at \SIrange{-59}{-43}{dB} and keep the separation of the reference. The joint reconstruction degrades only
    for a target on the cliff face, where the height changes by more than about \SI{100}{m} between two samples of the DEM phase
    (\SI{3.84}{m}); a finer DEM grid would remove this limit.
  \item The accuracy is limited by the DEM: the phase error grows as $\bxt\,\delta h$, and with a \SI{300}{m} baseline a DEM error of
        about \SI{2}{m} rms already raises the ambiguities to \SI{-30}{dB}.
  \item On straight tracks only $\Delta C_0$ matters, also for airborne geometries. A cross-track baseline that changes during the
        aperture by $\varepsilon(t)$ adds the phase $2\pi\varepsilon(t)\,h/(\lambda r_0\tan\theta)$ (the DEM phase with the baseline replaced by its change), mostly not a parabola; after a motion compensation to
        $h=0$ the ambiguity follows $20\log_{10}\varphi-\SI{9.5}{dB}$, so \SI{-40}{dB} needs residual phases below about \SI{0.02}{rad}, not
        $\pi/4$.
  \item Without noise none of the tested parameter-choice rules (L-curve with RelSea, discrepancy principle, Bayes minimum risk)
        improves on $\mu=0.03\,\sigma_{\max}$; at \SI{20}{dB} SNR the current solver loses about \SI{10}{dB} on the mountain, and the
        sparsity-promoting MARIA recovers it on point-target scenes, at two to five times the cost and with a sparse prior.
\end{itemize}

\section{Outlook}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Validation with realistic antenna patterns, orbits and noise, and eventually with real multistatic data.
  \item A topography-dependent motion compensation inside the model (a $\Delta C_0$ that changes along the aperture) for formations
        whose baselines vary in time, in particular airborne ones.
  \item Statistical, sparsity-promoting regularisation (MARIA-type) for noisy data, with safeguards for distributed scenes.
  \item An extension to two dimensions (azimuth and range), with the DEM height of every pixel, is the natural next step.
  \item Joint estimation of a DEM correction, using the unexplained part of the channels as the cost.
  \item Use of the blind-height condition (\cref{sec:blind}) as a design rule for the cross-track baselines of a formation.
  \item Computational cost: block-wise or GPU implementation of the model and its adjoint for full scenes.
\end{itemize}
