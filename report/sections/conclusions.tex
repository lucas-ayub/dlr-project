\chapter{Conclusions and outlook}\label{ch:conclusions}

\section{Conclusions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Cross-track baselines turn topography into a channel-dependent phase \eqref{eq:dc0}, proportional to $\bxt_{,i}h$, which the reconstruction filters of the reference geometry do not include. Without correction the median highest ambiguity of the sweeps is \SI{\SweepOneMedNone}{dB} in 1-D and
        \SI{\SweepTwoMedNone}{dB} in 2-D, against a reference near \SI{-48}{dB}.
  \item This phase is known from the DEM and the system geometry. The joint reconstruction places it inside the model of
        the channels, at every position of the focused line, and inverts model and topography together by regularised least
        squares with conjugate gradients. The model and its adjoint are applied with FFTs only, and the method reduces to the
        standard reconstruction on flat terrain.
  \item Using only the system parameters and the DEM, the joint reconstruction reaches a median of
        \SI{\SweepOneMedJoint}{dB} in 1-D (\SweepOneWithinTen\,\% of the runs within \SI{10}{dB} of the reference, about
        \SI{90}{\percent} for four or more channels) and \SI{\SweepTwoMedJoint}{dB} in 2-D, where with three or more channels
        it is within \SI{1}{dB} of the reference in the median.
  \item On real terrain (200 azimuth lines cut from the SRTM DEM of the Sierra Nevada, relief up to \SI{2930}{m}) the
        joint reconstruction lowers the median highest ambiguity from \SI{-8.3}{dB} to \SI{-41.5}{dB}, at the level of the
        reference (\SI{-41.6}{dB}), and is within \SI{10}{dB} of it in 199 of the 200 lines (\cref{sec:realdem}).
  \item The method works best with three or more channels and cross-track baselines that are not equally spaced, so that
        the channels see the folded parts of the spectrum with different phases. The accuracy of the DEM sets the final level:
        the phase error grows as $\bxt\,\delta h$, so long baselines call for accurate DEMs.
\end{itemize}

\section{Outlook}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Validation with realistic antenna patterns, orbits and baselines that vary along the orbit, and eventually with real
        multistatic data.
  \item 2-D: distributed scenes, layover and shadow, finer range resolution (envelope interpolation) and wide swaths
        (range-dependent kernels in blocks).
  \item Joint estimation of a DEM correction, using the unexplained part of the channels as the cost.
  \item Use of the conditioning analysis (\cref{sec:mismatch}) as a design rule for the cross-track baselines of a formation.
  \item Computational cost: block-wise or GPU implementation of the model and its adjoint for full scenes.
\end{itemize}
