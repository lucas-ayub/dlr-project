\chapter{Conclusions and outlook}\label{ch:conclusions}

\section{Conclusions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item With cross-track baselines, a height $h$ adds to channel $i$ the phase
        $\varphi_i\approx 2\pi\bxt_{,i}h/(\lambda r_0\tan\theta)$; the standard reconstruction cannot absorb it. On a
        \SI{1000}{m} mountain its highest ambiguity rises from \SI{-41}{dB} (flat) to \SI{-4.4}{dB}; on real terrain its
        median is \SI{-6.2}{dB}, against a reference near \SI{-40}{dB}.
  \item A per-channel correction can give only one phase to positions $X\approx\SI{6.2}{km}$ apart that share a Doppler
        bin. The joint reconstruction puts the DEM phase inside a matrix-free regularised inversion and, with the system
        parameters and the DEM only, reaches \SI{-40.8}{dB} on the mountain (reference \SI{-41.4}{dB}). It is within
        \SI{3}{dB} of the reference on seven of nine simulated terrains, six of seven real cuts and 130 of 150 random real
        lines, and never worse than the standard reconstruction.
  \item Failures are blind heights: a place and the place $X$ away get the same phase difference in every receiver, so the
        data cannot separate them. This explains all simulated failures and four of the ten failing real lines; the other
        six are not explained yet.
  \item Over mountain heights up to \SI{3}{km} (every \SI{100}{m}) it is within \SI{3}{dB} of the reference at 22 of 31
        heights, clearly hurt only at three near blind heights, and keeps the reference \SI{3}{dB} width (\SI{5.77}{m}).
  \item With 1 to 200 targets per line on six terrains the image error stays near \SI{-30}{dB}, set by the accuracy of
        $K\,H_i$; the 9 failures of 108 runs all involve a target near a blind height.
  \item Two targets on either side of a cliff up to \SI{1000}{m} stay at \SIrange{-59}{-43}{dB}; only a target on the cliff
        face, with more than about \SI{100}{m} of height change between two DEM samples (\SI{3.84}{m}), degrades it. A finer
        DEM grid would remove this limit.
  \item The DEM limits the accuracy: with a \SI{300}{m} baseline, about \SI{2}{m} rms of DEM error gives \SI{-30}{dB}.
  \item On straight tracks only $\Delta C_0$ matters, also for airborne geometries. A baseline change $\varepsilon(t)$ adds
        $2\pi\varepsilon(t)\,h/(\lambda r_0\tan\theta)$, mostly not a parabola; after motion compensation to $h=0$ the
        ambiguity follows $20\log_{10}\varphi-\SI{9.5}{dB}$, so \SI{-40}{dB} needs residual phases below about
        \SI{0.02}{rad}, not $\pi/4$.
  \item Without noise no tested parameter-choice rule (L-curve with RelSea, discrepancy, Bayes minimum risk) beats
        $\mu=0.03\,\sigma_{\max}$; at \SI{20}{dB} SNR the current solver loses about \SI{10}{dB} on the mountain, which
        MARIA recovers on point-target scenes at two to five times the cost.
\end{itemize}

\section{Outlook}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Validation with realistic antenna patterns, orbits and noise, and with real multistatic data.
  \item Topography-dependent motion compensation inside the model ($\Delta C_0$ varying along the aperture), in particular
        for airborne formations.
  \item Sparsity-promoting regularisation (MARIA-type) for noisy data, with safeguards for distributed scenes.
  \item Extension to two dimensions (azimuth and range), with the DEM height of every pixel.
  \item Joint estimation of a DEM correction from the unexplained part of the channels.
  \item The blind-height condition (\cref{sec:blind}) as a design rule for cross-track baselines.
  \item Block-wise or GPU implementation for full scenes.
\end{itemize}
