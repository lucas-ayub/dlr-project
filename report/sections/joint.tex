\chapter{Joint topography correction and reconstruction}\label{ch:joint}

The analysis of \cref{sec:sata-limit} shows that the correct phase (the $\dC$ read from the DEM) is already known; what
fails is applying it per channel before the parcels are separated. The joint reconstruction moves the DEM phase inside the
inversion.

\section{Joint reconstruction in the sub-apertures}

With the same sub-apertures as SATA, for every sub-aperture and every bin $\phi$ of the $\Nrx$ channel spectra the model
\eqref{eq:fold} is written with the phase of each parcel at its own ground position:
\begin{equation}
  S_i(\phi) = \sum_q H_i(f_q)\,\exp\!\Bigl(-\jj\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\Delta C_{0,i}(x_q)\Bigr)\,U_q,
  \qquad x_q = x_c + \frac{\lambda r_0}{2v} f_q ,
\end{equation}
and solved for the parcels $U_q$, which are then placed at their true Doppler, transformed back and overlap-added at the
full PRF. The steps are summarised in \cref{fig:flowsubap}. With $\dC=0$ this is the standard reconstruction ($\sim10^{-7}$); with one target it
equals the oracle. Its limit is the resolution of the sub-aperture: the DEM is read once per bin of a \SI{40}{ms} block, i.e.\
the terrain phase is taken as constant over about $\pm\SI{600}{m}$ of ground. A slope $s$ with a cross-track baseline is
equivalent to shifting the phase centre of the channel by $\bxt s/(2\tan\theta)$; when that shift is not small compared with
the block, the per-bin model breaks. With $\Delta\varphi=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,\frac{\bxt^{\max}\,s\,\delta x}{r_0\tan\theta}$
the phase change inside one SATA cell, the method stays within about \SI{10}{dB} of the reference for
$\Delta\varphi\lesssim\SI{0.5}{rad}$ and fails by 10 to \SI{35}{dB} above \SI{1}{rad}.

\section{Joint reconstruction over the whole line}

The final version removes the sub-apertures. The topographic residual of a scatterer is a constant phase over its whole
aperture (\cref{ch:model}); in the \emph{focused} full-rate line $g(x)$ it is therefore a pointwise multiplication. The
channels are modelled as
\begin{equation}
  \bm s_i = \mathcal D\Bigl\{ k_i * \bigl[\, g(x)\, \e^{-\jj\varphi_i(x)} \bigr] \Bigr\},
  \qquad \varphi_i(x)=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\Delta C_{0,i}\bigl(h(x)\bigr),
  \label{eq:jointmodel}
\end{equation}
where $\varphi_i(x)$ comes from the DEM only, $k_i$ is the response of channel $i$ to the reference
point (the azimuth chirp filtered by the reference-point filter $H_i$ of the standard reconstruction) and $\mathcal D$ is
the decimation to $\PRFop$ (\cref{fig:chainline}). Writing \eqref{eq:jointmodel} as $\bm s=\mathbf A\bm g$, the line is estimated by Tikhonov-regularised
least squares,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\bm g} = \arg\min_{\bm g}\ \sum_{i}\bigl\|\bm s_i-\mathbf A_i\bm g\bigr\|^2 + \mu^2\|\bm g\|^2 ,
\end{equation}
solved with conjugate gradients for least squares (CGLS) \cite{Hestenes1952,Trefethen1997}. Every iteration costs one
application of $\mathbf A$ and of its adjoint $\mathbf A\Hm$ (two FFTs per channel each). The regularisation is
$\mu=0.03\,\sigma_{\max}$, with $\sigma_{\max}$ estimated by a few power iterations, and the problem is solved in two passes
(iterated Tikhonov: the second pass solves for what the first one left), which turns the filter factor
$\sigma^2/(\sigma^2+\mu^2)$ into $1-(1-\sigma^2/(\sigma^2+\mu^2))^2$. Each pass runs up to 60 iterations. The output is
re-chirped with the reference response, $U=k*\hat g$, so that it has the same form as the standard reconstruction. The
complete algorithm is in \cref{fig:flowline}.

\section{Building the model and solving the system}\label{sec:joint-system}

This section derives the model \eqref{eq:jointmodel} from the echo of a single point, writes it as a linear system and
explains how that system is solved without ever forming its matrix.

\subsection{The model, step by step}

The full-rate line has $N=12288$ samples $n$ at $\PRF=\SI{2000}{Hz}$, one every $d=v/\PRF=\SI{3.84}{m}$ along track. Let
$k[n]$ be the monostatic echo of the reference point (the azimuth chirp) and $K(f)$ its DFT. The model is built by adding
one ingredient at a time.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{One target at the reference point, monostatic:} $u[n]=k[n]$, so $U(f)=K(f)$.
  \item \emph{The same target at $\ell=x/d$:} $u[n]=k[n-\ell]$, so $U(f)=K(f)\,\e^{-\jj2\pi f\ell/\PRF}$.
  \item \emph{Several targets} at $\ell_p$ with amplitudes $a_p$:
        \begin{equation}
          U(f)=\sum_p a_p K(f)\,\e^{-\jj2\pi f\ell_p/\PRF}=K(f)\,G(f),\qquad
          g[\ell]=\sum_p a_p\,\delta[\ell-\ell_p],
        \end{equation}
        where $G$ is the DFT of $g$. The focused line $g$ has one peak per target and is the unknown of the joint reconstruction.
  \item \emph{Several channels:} channel $i$ is the monostatic echo filtered by its transfer function \eqref{eq:Hi},
        $K_i(f)=H_i(f)\,K(f)$, so on flat terrain $S_i(f)=K_i(f)\,G(f)$.
  \item \emph{Relief:} a target at $\ell_p$ gets in channel $i$ the constant phase
        $\varphi_i[\ell_p]=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\Delta C_{0,i}(\ell_p)$ of \eqref{eq:dc0}:
        \begin{equation}
          S_i(f)=K_i(f)\sum_p a_p\,\e^{-\jj\varphi_i[\ell_p]}\,\e^{-\jj2\pi f\ell_p/\PRF}
                =K_i(f)\,\mathcal F\bigl\{\e^{-\jj\varphi_i}\odot g\bigr\}(f).
        \end{equation}
        The phase depends on the \emph{position} of the target (and on the channel), not on the frequency; it is therefore applied
        to $g$ as a pointwise ``screen'', before the DFT.
  \item \emph{Sampling of the channel:} the channel keeps one sample in $\Nrx$ of the full-rate signal,
        $s_i[k]=s_i^{\mathrm{full}}[\Nrx k]$. With $\mathbf F$ the DFT, $\mathbf K_i=\mathrm{diag}(K_i)$,
        $\bm\Theta_i=\mathrm{diag}(\e^{-\jj\varphi_i})$ and $\mathcal D$ the decimation,
        \begin{equation}
          \bm s_i=\underbrace{\mathcal D}_{\downarrow\Nrx}\ \underbrace{\mathbf F^{-1}}_{\text{IFFT}}\
          \underbrace{\mathbf K_i}_{\times K_i}\ \underbrace{\mathbf F}_{\text{FFT}}\
          \underbrace{\bm\Theta_i}_{\times\e^{-\jj\varphi_i}}\ \bm g=\mathbf A_i\bm g ,
          \label{eq:Ai}
        \end{equation}
        which is \eqref{eq:jointmodel} in matrix form. Stacking the channels gives $\bm s=\mathbf A\bm g$: with four
        channels, $4\times3072=12288$ equations for $12288$ unknowns.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The matrix is never built}

$\mathbf A$ is a $12288\times12288$ complex matrix: stored, it would take $12288^2\times\SI{16}{B}\approx\SI{2.4}{GB}$ and
each product would cost about $10^8$ multiplications. Every factor of \eqref{eq:Ai}, however, is either diagonal or a
fast transform, so $\mathbf A$ and $\mathbf A\Hm$ are applied as sequences of operations (\cref{fig:chainline}) and only
the diagonals are kept in memory (\cref{tab:operators}): about \SI{1.6}{MB} and eight FFTs per product with four channels.
$\mathbf A\Hm\mathbf A$ is never formed either; the solver only needs products with $\mathbf A$ and $\mathbf A\Hm$.

\paragraph{Why the system must be solved as a whole.} On flat terrain the screens are constant, $\mathbf F\bm\Theta_i\mathbf F^{-1}$
is a multiple of the identity and the only coupling between Doppler bins is the folding of $\mathcal D$, which links a bin
$f$ to $f\pm\PRFop,\ f\pm2\PRFop,\dots$ only. Ordered by bin, $\mathbf A$ then splits into $3072$ independent $4\times4$
blocks (rows: channels, columns: parcels): this is exactly the standard reconstruction \eqref{eq:fold}, the only place where
a matrix is actually assembled and inverted. With relief, $\mathbf F\bm\Theta_i\mathbf F^{-1}$ is a convolution in Doppler
with the spectrum of the screen and couples each bin with its neighbours as well. \Cref{fig:coupling} shows one column of
$\mathbf A\Hm\mathbf A$ for the example of \cref{fig:example1d}: on flat terrain a Doppler bin is coupled only with its
aliases, more than \SI{40}{dB} below the bin itself; with the mountain the coupling with the aliases rises to about
\SI{-22}{dB} and spreads over $\pm\SI{7}{Hz}$ (some 80 bins). The blocks no longer separate, and the whole line is solved
at once, iteratively.

\subsection{Solving the system}

The regularised problem and its normal equations are
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\bm g}=\arg\min_{\bm g}\ \|\mathbf A\bm g-\bm s\|^2+\mu^2\|\bm g\|^2
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \bigl(\mathbf A\Hm\mathbf A+\mu^2\mathbf I\bigr)\,\hat{\bm g}=\mathbf A\Hm\bm s ,
  \qquad \mu=0.03\,\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf A).
\end{equation}
$\sigma_{\max}$ is obtained by the power method, $\bm x\leftarrow\mathbf A\Hm\mathbf A\bm x/\|\mathbf A\Hm\mathbf A\bm x\|$
(12 iterations), $\sigma_{\max}\approx\sqrt{\|\mathbf A\Hm\mathbf A\bm x\|}$, which again uses only $\mathbf A$ and
$\mathbf A\Hm$. CGLS solves the normal equations without forming them (\cref{fig:flowline}): each iteration applies
$\mathbf A$ once, to the search direction $\bm p$, and $\mathbf A\Hm$ once, to the residual; $\alpha$ is the optimal step along
$\bm p$ and the new direction is conjugate to the previous ones, so no direction is searched twice. The second pass solves the
same problem for the residual left by the first and adds its solution to $\hat{\bm g}$ (iterated Tikhonov), which recovers
what $\mu$ damped where the channels do measure the signal well. On the example of \cref{fig:example1d} the two passes take
120 iterations in total.

Finally the raw full-rate line is restored, $U=K\odot\mathbf F\hat{\bm g}$, set to zero for $|f|>\Ba/2$ and transformed back
with $\mathbf F^{-1}$. This is the same raw line at \SI{2000}{Hz} that the standard reconstruction returns, so the azimuth
focusing that follows is identical for every method.

\section{Properties}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Consistency.} With a zero DEM the method reproduces the standard reconstruction within \SI{0.1}{\percent} rms.
  \item \textbf{Information used.} Only the channels, the system geometry and the DEM. The positions of the targets are never
        used (\cref{sec:sata-maps}).
  \item \textbf{Self-test of the DEM.} The part of the channels that the model does not explain is \SIrange{-41}{-39}{dB} with
        the right DEM and \SIrange{-20}{-7}{dB} with a wrong one (mirrored or shifted by \SI{1}{km}), which makes it a natural
        alarm for DEM errors.
  \item \textbf{Cost.} About \SI{3.3}{s} per line with four channels in the 1-D simulator (median over 680 runs), against
        \SI{0.15}{s} for SATA whole band.
  \item \textbf{Convergence.} \Cref{fig:convergence} follows the iterations on the example of \cref{fig:example1d}. The
        first iteration is the adjoint, i.e.\ the channels focused, corrected and summed (\SI{-10.2}{dB}, close to no SATA);
        about ten iterations bring the ambiguities below \SI{-39}{dB}, and the second pass gains another \SI{1}{dB}
        (\SI{-43.5}{dB} after $2\times60$ iterations). The unexplained part of the channels falls to \SI{-38}{dB} in the same
        way.
  \item \textbf{Ill-conditioned configurations.} Where the baselines do not separate the parcels (two channels, equally
        spaced baselines, certain heights with long baselines) the information is not in the data; the regularisation then
        leaves the ambiguity instead of amplifying noise.
\end{itemize}
